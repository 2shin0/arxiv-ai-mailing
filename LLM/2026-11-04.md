## 1. Adversarial Speculative Reasoning Improves Long Horizon Agents
- **Authors**: Hongyin Luo , Wei Fang , Lijie Yang , Owen Stepan , Daniel Suit , Jeff Krapf , Dana Wensberg , Jack O'Brien
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05345](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05345)
- **Abstract**:
> Speculative decoding has been treated purely as a systems optimization that speeds up large language model decoding without changing the model behavior. Under this view, the growing effort to train better draft models has been focused solely on acceptance rate. We argue that this view does not hold true for long-horizon agent reasoning. Measuring frontier open-weight models on complex agentic tasks with different speculative decoding approaches, we found that numerical noise grows within verification windows, and the draft acceptance results of the thinking process can change the output distribution of the model after 30 steps, thus noticeably influencing the reasoning accuracy of long-horizon agents in random directions. In this work, we improve the reasoning capability of target models by controlling such noise. While we cannot change the overall token distribution verified by the target model, we can encode critical reasoning with highest precision by training the draft model to be rejected. We identify these points in agent traces and train the draft model to favor adversarial preference. At inference the trained draft and the target part ways precisely at these decision points, verification restarts, so that critical thinking tokens are sampled from the target model and encoded under its least noisy conditions. This approach makes critical reasoning better indexed and more durable over a long horizon. On complex agentic coding benchmarks, our approach raises end-to-end success rate with no change to the target model or its output distribution. Our results recast the speculative decoding mechanism from a pure accelerator into an active allocator for context precision that improves the accuracy of long-horizon agents by training 0.06% to 0.28% of hosted parameters.

## 2. You don't need long context
- **Authors**: Peter Belcak , Saurav Muralidharan , Jindong Jiang , Yonggan Fu , Wonmin Byeon , Hanrong Ye , Ryan Angilly , Ali Taghibakhshi , Shih-Yang Liu , Siru Ouyang , Greg Heinrich , Pavlo Molchanov
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05306)
- **Abstract**:
> Later tokens are costlier to generate than earlier tokens. On frontier open weight models, the disparity in cost may rise to as much as 7x. Through frequent compaction, we show across a broad range of long-horizon and long-input tasks that frontier language models sacrifice little to no task accuracy when maintaining short contexts. We further demonstrate that LLM performance does not collapse on long-horizon software engineering tasks even when taking at most one action per context, and based on that we outline the concept of semantic state space models (SSSMs). Our findings question the need for long-context language models.

## 3. The Geometry of Ignorance: Detecting Suppressed Knowledge in LLMs through Activation Fragmentation
- **Authors**: Adar Peleg , Dvir Alsheich , Tomer Ashuach , Avi Mendelson
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05272](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05272)
- **Abstract**:
> Evaluating whether a large language model (LLM) genuinely knows a concept remains a fundamental problem, since behavioral metrics can be fooled by suppression: a model that refuses to answer may still harbor recoverable knowledge. The framework measures the geometric coherence of \textit{anon-vectors}, the differences in hidden states between a concept-specific query and its anonymized paraphrase, and shows that genuine knowledge forms a tight, low-entropy cluster while ignorance forces geometric fragmentation. Across ten instruction-tuned LLMs from three model families, all fragmentation metrics reliably separate known from unknown concepts, and post-unlearning, fragmentation geometry correlates with adversarial recovery across four attack types and five unlearning algorithms, though the direction of that relationship is shown to depend on attack methodology. Furthermore, a layer-resolved analysis reveals that different adversarial mechanisms exploit this geometry at varying depths, with high attack-specific correlations that are invisible to behavioral evaluation. Together, these results constitute a single-phase, attack-free proxy for unlearning robustness that captures aspects of parametric knowledge inaccessible to output-level metrics. ## 4. AgentAM: An AI Agent for Spatter- and Defect-Aware Process Optimization in Laser Powder Bed Fusion
- **Authors**: Olabode T. Ajenifujah , Amir Barati Farimani
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05171)
- **Abstract**:
> Predictive models for laser powder bed fusion (LPBF) are rarely used on the shop floor because querying them requires programming and model-specific knowledge. We present AgentAM, a large language model (LLM) agent that gives process engineers natural-language access to CFD-trained surrogates of spatter and melt pool geometry for SS316L through the Model Context Protocol (MCP). Polynomial surrogates trained on 279 single-track simulations predict melt pool dimensions ($R^2 \geq 0.95$) and spatter volume ($R^2 = 0.83$ on test data) at any power-velocity point in the trained range, and geometric criteria assign each point a geometry-derived defect regime (keyhole, lack of fusion, balling or stable) that matches the regime obtained from the simulated geometry in 93.4% of tracks under cross-validation. The agent selects and parameterizes tools for prediction, process-window scans and constrained optimization. Minimum-spatter optima often sat within 10 $\mu$m of the lack-of-fusion boundary; a margin-aware optimizer moved them away from all thresholds and raised the estimated probability of remaining classified Good from 0.94 to 1.00 under an assumed 2% process drift, and from 0.72 to 0.91 when jointly sampled cross-validated geometry residuals were added. About 40-50 well-spread simulations sufficed to reproduce the defect map. To evaluate the agent layer, we introduce a 145-prompt benchmark scored against direct tool calls, plus eight prompts (40 runs) on margin-aware requests. Over the 725 runs of the main benchmark the agent passed the intended inputs in 98.5% of parsing prompts, reported the scored spatter values and defect classes faithfully and met every stated constraint in the 65 feasible optimization runs, within a median 1.0% of the brute-force optimum.

## 5. Catalyzing Interactive HTML Generation Capabilities via High-Fidelity Reinforcement Learning
- **Authors**: Xujia Wang , Yixuan Liu , Shaohan Huang , Furu Wei , Lei Hou , Bin Xu , Juanzi Li
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05151](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05151)
- **Abstract**:
> Generating HTML from natural-language specifications is an important application of code large language models (LLMs). Despite producing renderable pages, current models often fail to ensure functionality and visual quality during actual interaction. Existing methods typically target task-specific webpage supervision, leaving demanding interactive artifact optimization underexplored. Using mini-games as a challenging testbed, we expose limitations in interaction experience, reward signal, and credit assignment fidelity that are obscured by shallow webpage interactions. We introduce WebCatalyst, a browser-interaction reinforcement learning framework with three complementary components: a game-level GUI assessor for sustained interaction and evaluation, Visual Anchor for mitigating VLM reward hacking, and Action-Trace Credit for grounding token-level credit assignment in runtime execution traces. Trained on fewer than 1K requests, predominantly for games, WebCatalyst raises the Cookie-Bench Mini Games score from 68.8 to 75.3 and also the WebGen-Bench accuracy from 48.8 to 56.9 on Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, outperforming Claude Opus 4.7 in average rank across evaluated metrics. Further analysis shows that Visual Anchor prevents aesthetic degradation, while runtime-grounded credit assignment accelerates functional improvement. Together, our results show that improving feedback fidelity is the key to substantially advancing interactive HTML generation.

## 6. Creating Power Flexibility in Datacenters via Query Routing
- **Authors**: Shivam Patel , Bingqing Chen , Jared Fernandez , Guannan Qu , Gauri Joshi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05148](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05148)
- **Abstract**:
> The growing energy requirements of large language model inference highlight the need for datacenters to support safe grid operation by providing power demand flexibility, while maintaining service for interactive workloads. This work investigates query routing as a mechanism to enable such capability by dynamically allocating queries across different language model instances without deferring requests. We develop a general analytical framework that connects query arrival rates and sequence length distributions to token iteration time, request residence time, serving capacity, and power consumption of model instances. Leveraging this framework, we characterize the feasible query routing allocations and the corresponding power demand flexibilities, enabling the selection of routing strategies to achieve desired power consumption behavior. We validate our framework across four GPU platforms on language models ranging from 1B to 70B parameters, obtaining 2.8% mean absolute percentage error in estimating achievable power flexibility. We evaluate our approach on a real trace-driven simulation of a 10,000 H100 GPUs datacenter, exhibiting flexibility up to 16% of total power consumption, with 90% of the desired power reduction achieved within approximately 10 seconds. These results establish query routing as an effective mechanism for datacenter power demand flexibility and demonstrate the broader applicability of our proposed framework to datacenter level planning and orchestration.

## 7. From Opinions to Commitments: Unstable Assignments and Silent Choices in AI-Mediated Proposals
- **Authors**: Sachit Mahajan , Javier Argota S{á}nchez-Vaquerizo , Arthur Capozzi , Dirk Helbing
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05135](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05135)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) are increasingly being used to turn citizens' opinions into collective proposals. These proposals can make assignments: who should act, what must be done, how much one should spend and by when. However, if the assignments change when the same opinions are processed again, publishing a single proposal can hide alternatives that participants never get to see. We examined this issue in four LLM mediators (three open-weight and one commercial) using opinions from DeliberationBank and Polis. For each task, a mediator drafted a proposal from thirty opinions five times, varying either the random seed or the order of the opinions. In 17% of five-run sets from the open-weight mediators and 20% from the commercial one, some runs contained assignments while others did not. Reordering the opinions changed the assignments almost as often as replacing them with opinions from different citizens. Three human annotators confirmed that the detected differences between runs were real, although the LLM judge overstated how often they occurred. Substantial variation remained under four safeguards: temperature zero, a commercial mediator, draft selection, and draft merging. The mediator's objective also shaped representation: prompts seeking agreement covered minority positions much less often than prompts asking to describe differences. A single proposal can therefore obscure choices in both the assignments it proposes and the views it excludes. We propose audits that make alternative assignments visible and disclose the objective behind each proposal.

## 8. LatentTriad: Pre-Action Risk Representations for Malicious Agent Skill Detection
- **Authors**: Zhe Yu , Yi Liu , Ying Zhang , Simin Chen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05088)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model agents are increasingly extended with reusable skills, but untrusted skill content can induce operations outside the user's task. Detecting this contextual risk requires more than suspicious-pattern matching, while dynamic analysis incurs the cost of skill execution. We find that hidden states formed during prefill contain readable information about task-operation consistency, including across held-out expression forms in controlled evaluations. Matched Operation-state interventions also affect later Boundary states and shift fixed risk readouts toward the donor label. Based on these findings, we introduce LatentTriad, which combines contextual Boundary features, group-adjusted Operation directions, and departures from benign state relationships. All branches share one prefill: detection generates no output tokens, invokes no tools, and executes no skill code. Across 5,830 skill packages from five sources and seven backbones, LatentTriad achieves 99.0-99.2% pooled F1 and 93.0-94.6% source-macro F1. A separately measured compact implementation completes prefill and detection in a median of 259-284 ms. These results connect pre-action risk readability and functional influence to execution-free skill screening.

## 9. ALBI: LLM-Guided Inductive Logic Programming via Iterative Generate-Test-Constrain Refineme
- **Authors**: Aboubaker Seddiq Benterki , Jüri Vain , Gert Kanter , Leelo Aino Jürgenstein
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05039)
- **Abstract**:

## 13. Where Is the Truth When AI Agents Disagree? Dependence-Aware Belief Propagation for Unsupervised Truth Discovery (Extended Version)
- **Authors**: Laure Berti-Equille
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04805)
- **Abstract**:
> LLM agents often give conflicting answers to the same question, and many deployed agents are variants of one base model, so their errors are correlated. We study truth discovery among such agents: inferring the true answer and each agent's reliability without labels. Classical truth-discovery and crowdsourcing estimators reward an agreeing bloc and can fall below majority vote. We propose BP-TD, which adds per-agent decorrelation weights to mean-field variational inference on a one-coin Dawid-Skene model, so that agents sharing a base model count together as about one vote. Its first iteration is exactly a weighted vote; later iterations estimate agent reliability. With 13 local agents on MMLU, BP-TD beats majority vote without any labels (0.716 vs. 0.624 on 160 questions; 0.707 vs. 0.674 on 10,000) and ranks agents by their true accuracy, which classical estimators invert. The gain depends on the pool: over 2,852 sub-pools of four benchmarks, decorrelation helps when the largest correlated bloc is weaker than the other agents and hurts when it is the strongest, and we found no label-free statistic that tells the two cases apart. Labels change the picture quickly: with 20 labelled questions, selecting the single best agent is more accurate than every aggregator we tested on MMLU, ARC and TruthfulQA.

## 16. When Should an LLM Steering Edit Depend on the Input?
- **Authors**: Rachael Harkness , Konrad Rawlik
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04757](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04757)
- **Abstract**:
> An activation steering edit should depend on the input exactly when the destination varies \emph{inside} the class a query requests, and that is measurable before any rule is fitted or applied. Contrastive Activation Addition (CAA) adds one fixed direction to every input, the difference of the class means. Conditioning it on the requested class accounts for the between-class variation in the required displacement and leaves the within-class variation untouched, which no rule that reads only the source state can express. We study the impact of increasing input-dependence in the steering edit, moving from a difference of means to a destination retrieved per query --- the mean of the $k$ stored states nearest the query, inside the requested class --- in which the two baselines are endpoints: at $k$ equal to the class size the retrieved rule \emph{is} the class-conditional difference of means. We measure input-dependent steering on six substitution corpora that vary in whether the edit changes a word or a clause, and on an external benchmark, and find that input-dependent steering outperforms fixed-direction steering when the destination varies within the class. Retrieval wins at every layer of every substitution corpus in two models, and the level of input-dependence needed governs how to apply it: word-level edits are best made in the first tenth of the network from a few neighbours, clause-level edits at any depth from the class mean. Where the class already determines the answer the class mean suffices and retrieval only matches it, though a fixed direction still fails there. Four quantities computed from the states alone identify the regime in which the edit should be applied.

## 18. A research world model of power engineering learns its dynamics and reveals what-ifs

## 21. Learnings and Pitfalls from AI-Assisted A/B Experimentation at Scale
- **Authors**: Brett Daley , Ilya Gorbachev , Anubhav Natani , Nicholas Brown , Athena Dai , Rachel Song , Marcin Pekalski , Josip Domazet , Anchal Choubey , Daniel Ting
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04515](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04515)
- **Abstract**:
> A/B testing underpins product decisions by providing trustworthy, reproducible quantification of proposed changes' effects. Growing large language model (LLM) capabilities have motivated efforts to automate experimentation workflows. Yet LLMs are nondeterministic and prone to hallucination, properties that conflict with the trust experimentation systems must provide. Experiments are also operationally messy: diagnosing anomalies, debugging configurations, and conducting bespoke follow-up analyses require flexible reasoning and manual effort. - **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04169)
- **Abstract**:
> Why do language models fail on long inputs? The common answer is length. We show that, for retrieval, length is cheap and competition is not. On five open models (0.5B to 3B parameters, four families), neutral filler costs nothing: accuracy stays at 0.97 to 1.00, up to 32K tokens on Qwen2.5-1.5B. Statements in the format of the evidence, placed at the same positions, drop it to 0.16 to 0.33. A single statement that gives the queried key a rival value multiplies the loss by 9 to 110, and every additional rival adds to it. Inside the model, one quantity tracks the failure: the attention mass left on the evidence. Across 126,473 trials it explains up to 58% of the variance in loss; log length explains at most 8%. The masked-filler protocol behind the claim that length alone hurts reproduces on MMLU (11 to 18 points lost at 30K tokens), but fails a visible control in retrieval: masking costs 45 accuracy points at 32K tokens, while the same filler left visible costs none. In retrieval, context rot is a competition for attention. We release the probe and all 126K trial-level measurements.

## 102. Swapping the Judge Changes the Ranking: A Preregistered Study of Cross-Provider Replication in LLM-Judged System Comparisons
- **Authors**: Sangdo Han (CEO Business School), Moonsoo Kim (CEO Business School)
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04090](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.04090)
- **Abstract**:
> Practitioners who compare systems with an LLM judge and have no human labels often check a conclusion by repeating it with a judge from another provider. We test how far that check can be trusted. In a preregistered study (with a disclosed post-registration reduction of the judge set), five judge models from three providers re-judged 4,000 Chatbot Arena battles (nine systems, 33 system pairs) that carry human votes, each in both response orders. Agreement with the human vote was low at the verdict level (median Cohen's kappa = 0.22), and system rankings did not survive a change of judge: Kendall's tau with the human ranking ranged from 0.17 to 0.89 (median 0.67), so the preregistered hypothesis that rankings remain stable was not supported. Conclusions replicated by two low-cost judges from different providers matched the human conclusion more often than single-judge conclusions (+0.10 precision), but the gain came from filtering out one poorly aligned judge, and replication involving that judge left a conclusion for only 35% of comparisons. Judges that agreed with humans reached a stable conclusion with no more verdicts than human votes required; the others failed to converge on 26-70% of system pairs at the sample sizes tested (up to 100 verdicts per pair). Disagreements with the human vote co-occurred across judges far more often than independence predicts, although a single human vote per battle cannot separate shared judge bias from voter noise.

## 103. Measure Before You Repair: An Explainable Agent for Blind Restoration of Multi-Artifact 3-D MRI
- **Authors**: Necati Sefercioglu , Mehmet Ozan Unal , Metin Ertas , Altan Cakir , Isa Yildirim
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03996](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03996)
- **Abstract**:

We present Dx-Rx, an agent in which a multimodal large language model (MLLM) measures suspected artifacts before it repairs them. To our knowledge, it is the first agent to restore whole 3-D MRI volumes, whereas earlier MRI agents restore 2-D slices. ## 104. Whose startup does the AI back? An algorithm audit of reputation and demographic signals in LLM-assisted crowdfunding
- **Authors**: Mirza Samad Ahmed Baig , Syeda Anshrah Gillani
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03993](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03993)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) assistants increasingly advise backers on which crowdfunding campaigns to support. LLMs have known race and gender problems, and it is unsettled whether they reproduce, erase, or reverse the discrimination documented in entrepreneurial finance. We ask when, and through which decision process, LLM assistants use founders' gender and ethnicity. ## 106. Balancing Chain-of-Thought and Answer Supervision for Small Language Models on Medical Question Answering
- **Authors**: Le Zhou , Xiao Luo , Chinmay Chandra , Zhan Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03948)
- **Abstract**:
> Small language models (<=9B parameters) can support local medical question answering (QA) while reducing privacy and latency concerns, but effective fine-tuning remains challenging. This paper compares two chain-of-thought (CoT) supervised fine-tuning methods: Uniform-across-Sequence (UaS), which applies equal loss weighting to the full CoT-and-answer sequence, and Segment-weighted (SwS), which separately weights CoT and answer losses. We evaluate six general- and medical-domain models on multiple-choice QA (MedQA and EMS-MCQA) and free-text QA (Med-O1). We also find that excessively long CoT generation is associated with lower performance in some models on either multiple-choice or free-text QA tasks, suggesting that limiting the CoT length during training may benefit selected models. Overall, the results show that CoT loss weighting should be matched to the task format and the knowledge already encoded in the base model, rather than using a single fine-tuning strategy for all clinical QA tasks.

## 107. Why Pay for Long Reasoning When Rewrite Do Trick? Causal Effects of Reasoning History Compaction on Future Generation and Agent Cost
- **Authors**: Anton Hristov
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03852](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03852)
- **Abstract**:
> Reasoning substantially improves language models' ability to solve difficult problems, but long reasoning traces make these gains expensive. Efficient reasoning should express useful analysis in fewer tokens by reducing repetition and text that adds little value to solving the problem. Progress in proprietary models motivates this goal, yet their hidden scratchpads limit direct study of how it is achieved. We investigate whether open-weight models can generate less verbose reasoning by changing the form of their own earlier thoughts, without retraining. Our intervention rewrites prior reasoning into compact state records before pending tool results are revealed, then compares continuations from original and compact histories. These findings identify reasoning history as one way to reduce future reasoning-token use and motivate model-specific compaction policies evaluated for both efficiency and retained capability.

## 111. RuntimeTickets: Source-Grounded Security Obligations for Agent Skills
- **Authors**: Kuiyi Gao , Xue Liu , Bowei He
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03772](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03772)
- **Abstract**:
> Third-party Agent Skills extend what an autonomous agent can do, but a malicious Skill can hide data exposure or output manipulation inside a legitimate workflow. Neither reading a Skill's description nor granting it tool permissions tells whether its execution meets the user's security requirements. We introduce RuntimeTickets, a protocol that enforces and audits these requirements while a Skill runs. From released malicious-Skill datasets we derive a risk model and build adversarial-benign Skill pairs. From the task's delivery contract, a language model proposes security obligations for that task. Each obligation cites the Skill lines it answers, and the ticket tightens the host's approvals into fine-grained allowed actions but never loosens them. ## 117. Contextual Linear Activation Steering of Language Models
- **Authors**: Brandon Hsu , Daniel Beaglehole , Adityanarayanan Radhakrishnan , Mikhail Belkin
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24693)
- **Abstract**:
> Linear activation steering is a powerful approach for controlling and specializing large language models by adding linear representations of concepts to their activations at inference. While effective, existing methods often steer with a fixed strength, independent of how much steering any given input requires. In this work, we introduce Contextual Linear Activation Steering (CLAS), which decomposes activation steering into a fixed steering direction and a context-dependent steering coefficient. This decomposition preserves the interpretability of the steering direction while enabling the steering strength to vary with context. It also enables CLAS to perform selective steering: steering multiple behaviors across different portions of a single generation, which cannot be achieved using a fixed steering strength. In addition, across a wide range of steering benchmarks and language models, CLAS consistently outperforms standard linear activation steering and matches or exceeds ReFT and LoRA in settings with limited labeled data. Notably, CLAS achieves this using directions that also serve as reliable concept monitors, a property that directions learned via fine-tuning lack. We therefore propose CLAS as an interpretable and accurate method for controlling and specializing large language models.
