## 1. BEAKER: An Expert-Curated Benchmark for Embodied Brains in Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories
- **Authors**: Fei Lin , Tengchao Zhang , Ziyang Gong , Xiaotong Yu , Bohan Zhang , Yifan Zhou , Qihao Yang , Ji Dai , Dong Li , Yining Jiang , Qinghua Ni , Jun Huang , Qiang Zhang , Yue Zhou , Zhihang Zhong , Yonglin Tian , Fei-Yue Wang , Xue Yang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06911)
- **Abstract**:
> Self-Driving Chemical Laboratories (SDCLs) are moving chemical experimentation toward embodied automation, where machines must perceive laboratory scenes, reason about experimental states, and act under physical, procedural, and safety constraints. Although Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) are increasingly considered as embodied brains for such systems, existing laboratory benchmarks mainly focus on safety, anomaly detection, or general scientific understanding, leaving their execution-oriented embodied capabilities underexplored. To fill this gap, we introduce BEAKER, a Benchmark for evaluating Embodied Actionability, Knowledge, Experimentation, and Reasoning in SDCLs. BEAKER contains 1,000 expert-curated question-answer samples across 11 image and video task types, covering key judgments required for chemical laboratory execution, including affordance grounding, trajectory planning, equipment, state, and safety recognition, apparatus setup, operation and step reasoning, and temporal procedure reasoning. All questions and annotations are manually created and reviewed by chemistry and embodied Artificial Intelligence (AI) experts, without AI-assisted generation or annotation. We evaluate 28 general-purpose, science-oriented, chemistry-specialized, and embodied MLLMs in an offline, single-turn setting. Results show that current models can follow coarse experimental procedures in videos, but still struggle to ground actions into operable regions and feasible trajectories, to recognize fine-grained laboratory apparatus, states, and safety norms, and to order steps in mid-length procedures. BEAKER thus reveals a clear gap between coarse procedural understanding and the precise, action-grounded perception required for laboratory execution, providing a diagnostic benchmark for future MLLMs in SDCLs.

## 2. Dull, Dirty, Dangerous? Working Conditions and the Limits of Occupational AI Exposure
- **Authors**: Siyi Yu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06888](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06888)
- **Abstract**:
> Automation is often justified as relief from dull, dirty, and dangerous work. I examine how these conditions relate to occupational AI exposure using 21 O*NET items across 754 US occupations. Higher exposure is associated with lower condition scores under a large language model (LLM) measure and AI Occupational Exposure (AIOE), with equal occupation weights and employment weights. Adjustment for physical demands, required education, training and experience, and wages reduces the composite association. The adjusted confidence intervals include zero for LLM exposure and remain below zero for AIOE. Separating repetition from restricted discretion also changes what the combined Dull score conveys. These comparisons show how item groupings and occupational characteristics shape the interpretation of exposure estimates. Occupational profiles offer a starting point for selecting contrasting research settings. Establishing desired automation or realized relief requires evidence about workers' preferences and specific interventions.

## 4. CompressionBench: How can your coding agents compress LLMs?
- **Authors**: Xinrui Zhong , Zhifeng Li , Zhiming Wang , Yongqi Liu , Shanglin Yang , Yang Zhou , Zikun Li , Zhuoming Chen , Beidi Chen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06858)
- **Abstract**:
> Recursive self-improvement requires AI systems to improve the models and infrastructure used by subsequent systems. LLM compression offers a concrete efficiency objective: retain a pretrained model's behavior within a smaller model storage budget. We study whether coding agents can carry out this step autonomously. We formulate compression as a terminal task in which an agent receives a source checkpoint, storage limit, inference environment, and time budget, then constructs compressed weights and executable inference code. The agent controls its algorithm and experiments; perplexity feedback guides search, and independent verification checks the final model. Across six agents and six model--size settings, 29 of 36 submissions pass verification. Selecting the lowest-perplexity agent design per task yields an average 3.4% perplexity increase over BF16 and 77.33% downstream accuracy, compared with 79.56% for BF16. Recorded experiments reveal how agents achieve these outcomes and where improvement breaks down. Agents lower PPL in 21 of 24 revisions at nearly fixed storage; complete-model feedback reverses 17 of 79 prior local rankings; and generation and inference tests expose failures that likelihood alone misses. The results identify representation design, experimental judgment, and capability validation as central to agent-driven model compression, a measurable component of efficiency-oriented self-improvement.

## 5. ServerBench: How can your coding agents design specialized LLM serving systems?
- **Authors**: Xinrui Zhong , Zhiming Wang , Yongqi Liu , Zhaohui Wang , Shanglin Yang , Zikun Li , Zhuoming Chen , Beidi Chen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06841](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06841)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) coding agents develop software through experimentation, offering an opportunity to design the serving systems supporting their own operation. Effective serving requires coordinating implementation and operating decisions under session dependencies, latency constraints, and correctness requirements. We introduce ServerBench, a benchmark for agentic design of complete, specialized LLM serving systems. Agents experiment against a common objective, dollars per dollar: latency-qualified token value per dollar of GPU cost. Stateful replay preserves session dependencies, and independent verification checks the submitted system. Across 11 coding-agent models and three deployments, Claude Sonnet 5.5 achieves 2.97 dollars per dollar on Gemma E4B, $4.87\times$ the vLLM reference; 22 of 33 runs yield verified systems, and 13 exceed the strongest baseline. Our analysis identifies three findings: high-value designs coordinate capacity improvements with load selection; agents interpret performance feedback through workload-relevant experiments; and reliable delivery requires verification of the execution paths introduced by optimization. Overall, ServerBench supports systematic study of how agents translate service objectives and feedback into effective, verified serving systems for given task specifications.

## 6. Task-Efficient Agent Evaluation Using Cached Traces
- **Authors**: Ben Johnson , Hayden Helm
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06803](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06803)
- **Abstract**:
> Efficient benchmarking estimates a system's full benchmark performance from a small number of task executions. Standard approaches rely solely on the binary correctness of executions from a collection of already-evaluated reference systems. An agentic system, however, produces a rich trace for each execution that can reveal substantially more about its capabilities. Leveraging this information requires useful trace representations. We propose representing a trace by extracting a structured summary of it with an LLM and embedding the summary with a standard text encoder. These representations preserve correctness- and task-related signal while minimizing the presence of system identity-, model family- and harness-related signals that dominate raw trace embeddings. Given this representation for each task execution from all reference systems, we construct low-dimensional representations of the systems and predict a new system's score from its position among the reference systems. On SWE-bench Verified and Terminal-Bench 2.0, augmenting an item-response model with this geometry-based prediction reduces mean absolute error from 0.13 to 0.08 (Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-4}$) and from 0.14 to 0.12 (Wilcoxon $p < 10^{-3}$), respectively, when provided a single random executed task. Further, given access to the full set of reference systems, our approach dominates baselines for any given number of executed tasks on both benchmarks.

## 9. Addition by Subtraction: Toward Mitigating Fragile Safety Alignment in Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Hamid Dashtbani , Alexandra Dmitrienko
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06553)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) undergo safety alignment to prevent harmful generations, yet they remain vulnerable to jailbreak attacks. Recent work reveals a deeper structural weakness: safety alignment can be highly sparse, such that pruning a small number of safety-critical neurons or ablating a single refusal direction can substantially undermine aligned behavior. In this work, we take the first steps toward strengthening safety alignment such that it is less dependent on sparse model components. We propose Addition by Subtraction, which gradually removes identified safety-critical neurons during finetuning and uses preference optimization to re-establish safety-aligned behavior through other parts of the model. Our results show that the resulting models are substantially more robust to pruning-based attacks. Remarkably, these benefits generalize beyond the white-box attacks motivating our design: Addition by Subtraction improves robustness against other sparsity-based interventions, including refusal-direction ablation, as well as a broad range of black-box jailbreak attacks. The finetuned models also exhibit improved truthfulness while largely preserving general capabilities. Comprehensive experiments across four models from three vendors demonstrate consistent improvements in safety robustness, reducing attack success rates by up to 90% for some attacks.

## 10. Tools Need Not Speak in Text: Latent Context Compression for Agents

- **Abstract**:

## 13. Does Context Compression Preserve Refusal Alignment?

- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06380](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06380)
- **Abstract**:
> Context compression reduces inference cost by encoding inputs into compact representations while preserving semantic content. ## 17. SafeStart: Mitigating the Overlooked Safety Cost of Active Expert Reduction in MoE LLM Serving
- **Authors**: Erfan Baghaei Potraghloo , Saeid Shokoufa , Seyedarmin Azizi , Arya Fayyazi , Souvik Kundu , Mehdi Kamal , Massoud Pedram
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06147)
- **Abstract**:
> Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) language models send each token through a few of many expert subnetworks. Serving methods make these models faster by running even fewer experts per token, and they are validated on accuracy benchmarks. We show that these methods can weaken the model's refusal of harmful requests, and we introduce SafeStart, which largely restores refusal while keeping most of the speedup. In vLLM on one RTX A6000, keeping about two thirds of the selected experts speeds up offline decoding by 15 to 28% at batch size 8. We find that, at this setting, under a jailbreak that tells the model not to refuse, the share of harmful requests that the model answers rises by 18.4 percentage points on Qwen1.5-MoE, 9.5 on OLMoE, and 6.6 on DeepSeek-V2-Lite, the three of nine models where it rises significantly, while MMLU accuracy drops by less than two points. We discover that most of this increase arises while the model processes the prompt and generates its first few tokens. SafeStart therefore runs all experts for the prompt and the first eight generated tokens, and the reduced set of experts afterwards, through a fused kernel in vLLM. With experts removed, SafeStart keeps at least 85% of the offline decode speedup at batch sizes 8 and 32 and brings the change under this jailbreak to between -2.1 and +1.3 points for three expert-reduction methods on these three models. On 889 held-out requests, in an analysis fixed before generation, truncation raises harmful compliance by 5.8 to 21.4 points, and SafeStart leaves a rise of 0.6 to 2.6 points, non-inferior to the original model on two of the three models. Our results show that faster MoE serving need not cost safety: a serving system can keep most of the gain from running fewer experts by running all of them at the start of each response, and it should test refusal, not only accuracy, before it enables such a method.

## 19. Reasoning-Trace Overflow in Code Generation: An Empirical Study of Explicit Reasoning Models
- **Authors**: Lukas Twist , Helen Yannakoudakis , Jie M. Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06095](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06095)
- **Abstract**:
> Explicit reasoning can help language models generate code, but it also introduces a practical risk: the model may spend so much of its output budget reasoning that it never produces usable code. We study this behaviour in relatively small open-weight reasoning models, framing it as reasoning-trace overflow. Across seven models and six code benchmark settings, we measure how much of a fixed 32k-token output budget is spent on reasoning, whether that reasoning improves pass@1, and whether lightweight inference-time controls can mitigate overflow. Our results show that the value of reasoning is highly conditional. Overflow is rare on simpler function-level tasks, but becomes common on harder code-generation benchmarks and for particular model families: on several model--benchmark pairs, more than half of generations exhaust the full 32k-token budget while still reasoning. Most complete overflow is associated with repetitive reasoning loops, although substantial long reasoning also occurs without looping. Disabling reasoning can therefore be competitive for overflow-prone models, simply because it ensures that an answer is produced. At the same time, reasoning remains valuable for stronger models on harder algorithmic and code-understanding tasks. Finally, prompt steering can substantially reduce overflow for some models but is inconsistent, while budgeted transitions provide more direct control and can improve both performance and token efficiency for overflow-prone models. These findings suggest that explicit reasoning should be treated as a controllable resource for code generation, rather than an always-on capability.

## 21. Graph-Mediated Large Language Model Systems in the Agentic Era: Reasoning, Knowledge, Retrieval, Execution, and Coordination
- **Authors**: Haihui Liu , Yang Chen , Zice Qiu , Yun Zhang , Jialun Zhong , Wenhua Wang , Yanzeng Li , Tian Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05953](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05953)
- **Abstract**:
> Graphs play an increasingly important role in large language model (LLM) systems, supporting structural reasoning, knowledge grounding, retrieval, and memory. As these systems evolve toward the agentic era, graphs also organize execution dependencies and multi-agent communication, linking intermediate observations and retained information to subsequent decisions. However, understanding their contribution requires distinguishing the function of each graph operation, the component responsible for its computation, and the subsequent use of its output. This survey introduces an operation-level role--locus taxonomy for reasoning, knowledge and evidence, retrieval and memory, execution, and coordination. Large language models (LLMs) promise NPCs that converse openly, adapt to new situations, and behave in ways developers did not fully author, prompting a wave of research and commercial experimentation. Yet the field lacks a unified picture of what has been built, how well it works, and at what cost. We address this gap with a systematic mapping study of 377 papers from the academic literature, yielding 42 papers analysed against a main research question and ten sub-questions across four thematic axes. We provide the first quantitative characterisation of component adoption, latency, safety coverage, and reproducibility: short-term memory and world-state grounding are near-universal (60% and 69%), whereas long-term memory (31%), reflection (38%), and retrieval-augmented generation (14%) remain rare, and response times span 2.6 to 15 seconds. Finally, we identify six structural barriers to commercial adoption and offer evidence-based recommendations for developers and researchers.

## 82. A Training-Free Socket for DNA in Frozen Language Models
- **Authors**: Arash Fatehi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05401](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05401)
- **Abstract**:
> Connecting a DNA foundation model to a large language model (LLM) usually requires training a connector, and often the LLM too. We show that a closed-form map is enough for gene-level questions. Our method, the socket, fits one ridge regression (the plug) from frozen Evo 2 features to the host LLM's own representation of the gene's symbol, name, and description at an early layer. The plug's output replaces the hidden state at placeholder positions, and no gradient passes through the host. We test it on GeneHop, a five-way multiple-choice benchmark split by homology. In a single run on the held-out test set, under a frozen analysis plan, real DNA beats shuffled DNA on all four hosts: by +22.2 points [19.8, 25.0] on Qwen3.5-4B, +26.1 [24.3, 27.9] on Qwen3.6-27B, +16.7 [14.2, 19.7] on Mistral-7B, and +18.4 [16.2, 21.0] on OLMo-2-7B. All four survive Holm correction, every null control stays within two points of the sham level, and the development results agree within 2.3 points. A layer rule that uses no task items was selected on three hosts; it predicted the working layer of the fourth before any DNA-plug scoring on it. A text-only linear map lets one plug feed any other host, at a cost of at most 0.9 points on test. The socket conveys gene identity: nearest-neighbour retrieval recovers 72-84% of the effect, but the plug beats it on every host by 3.0-5.2 points and keeps most of its effect without training homologs.

## 83. Do agents need to sleep?
- **Authors**: Ido Haber , Giulio Tononi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02216](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02216)
- **Abstract**:
> LLM-based agents, which increasingly operate persistently across sessions and accumulate memory through interaction, already exhibit the familiar symptoms of unchecked plasticity: semantic drift, degraded retrieval precision, propagation of stored errors, and saturation of memory capacity. Drawing on the selectionist "ringing, resonance and renormalization" (RRR) account of sleep, we propose that each of its three phases maps onto an operation feasible in digital memory architectures. Engineers building stateful agents, meanwhile, are independently rediscovering the problems sleep evolved to solve: what to forget, and when to do the forgetting. From this we derive the falsifiable prediction that selectionist consolidation will outperform instructionist alternatives as memory stores grow large. The core claim concerns the nature of continuous learning itself: plasticity has a price, and for any system that learns continuously under finite capacity while coupled to its environment, that price is sleep.
