## 1. ACCT: Asymmetric Cross-LLM Context Transfer
- **Authors**: Yuzhe Liu , Haolin Tian , Tonghan Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42116)
- **Abstract**:
> In real-world deployments, large language model (LLM) systems are increasingly evolving from single-model invocation toward heterogeneous deployments in which different models are dynamically invoked for inference. When the same context is reused across models, however, each newly selected target model typically has to process it again, repeatedly incurring substantial computational costs. Therefore, we propose ACCT (Asymmetric Cross-LLM Context Transfer), which targets KV cache transfer between models of different scales within the same model family. ACCT transforms the KV cache produced by a source model into sparse KV states that can be directly consumed by a target model, thereby enabling cross-model reuse of context computation and decoupling context computation from downstream inference. ACCT consists of three lightweight components: the Cartographer identifies query-relevant context tokens for each target layer and KV head; the Allocator determines how many context tokens each target layer and KV head retains under a shared total KV budget; and the Compiler transforms selected source KV into sparse target KV. The target model can therefore perform inference using the transferred KV without reprocessing the original context. We evaluate ACCT in two complementary settings comprising five general-capability benchmarks and six highly context-dependent tasks. Experiments show that ACCT matches or exceeds the target model's original inference performance even when reducing the KV cache size by 75%. At a context length of approximately 19K tokens, ACCT's KV conversion achieves a 6.5-21.2 times speedup over target context prefill. These results demonstrate the feasibility of transferring context computation across models under a limited KV budget.

## 2. Same Question, Different Answers: Auditing Persona-Dependent Variation in Six LLMs on Smoking-Cessation Advice
- **Authors**: Artin Yousefi , Aizierjiang Aiersilan , Lorien C Abroms , David A Broniatowski
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42039](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42039)
- **Abstract**:
> Hundreds of millions of people now ask large language model (LLM) chatbots for health advice, which raises a question that is simple to pose and hard to answer: does everyone get the same advice? We audit six widely deployed LLMs from four providers on smoking cessation, a domain with codified clini- cal guidance and substantial public-health stakes, by putting the same four cessation questions to twelve user personas that differ in life stage, self-described expertise, and smoking history, for a total of 2,880 responses. What the models say depends on who appears to be asking. Every model named FDA-approved prescription cessation medications less often to teen personas than to adult personas, by 32 to 67 percentage points, and in five of six models a persona presenting health- care expertise received fewer facts than a persona presenting none. We then evaluate one deployable intervention: a short system prompt specifying a factual floor, the set of core med- ical facts every user should receive when they are relevant, while leaving tone, vocabulary, and framing free to adapt. The prompt narrows persona differences in every model, but its effect on overall fact coverage varies in both magnitude and direction across models, so a prompt validated on one system cannot be assumed to transfer to another. Two cautions frame these results. Equal mention rates are not always the correct clinical target, because prescription cessation pharmacother- apy is not recommended for minors, so a defensible factual floor must be age-aware rather than uniform. And because our personas vary on several attributes at once, we report scenario- level differences rather than isolated effects of age or expertise. We release all 2,880 responses and the analysis code.

## 3. TradeWar: A Benchmark for Execution-Grounded Trade Negotiation with Adaptive LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Xiangyu Kong , Ruirui Yang , Xia Hou
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42004](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42004)
- **Abstract**:
> Multi-turn negotiation remains a challenging problem for large language model (LLM) agents. While LLMs can conduct natural-language dialogue, existing benchmarks predominantly assess only agreement rates, overlooking whether negotiated commitments actually create value once executed. To address this gap, we introduce TradeWar, a multi-country simulation benchmark that translates negotiation dialogues into concrete tariff and trade-share policies, enforces capacity and network constraints during settlement, and evaluates outcomes via an Economic Health Index (EHI). To solve this execution-grounded task, we propose TACTIC (Trade-aware Adaptive Consolidation and Theory-of-mind for International Commerce), an agent that iteratively refines its negotiation strategy across episodes-without updating base LLM weights-by leveraging prior experience, opponent behavior, and past settlement outcomes. Despite using zero-shot chain-of-thought (CoT) prompting, PromptLLM attains a 75% agreement rate but only 57.1 final EHI (*100), whereas TACTIC reaches 68.8. Across 20 sequential episodes and three seeds, TACTIC's final EHI improves from 58.2 to 73.2, compared with 65.2 for an ablation without cross-episode updates. Under technology-controls and energy-crisis shocks, TACTIC also exhibits smaller performance degradation than PromptLLM and MemoryLLM. These results indicate that CoT deliberation and interaction memory alone do not fully address delayed execution feedback, whereas settlement-grounded adaptation improves long-horizon negotiation in TradeWar.

## 4. Prune Gradually, Adapt Sparsely: Efficient Pruning of Large Language Models at Extreme Sparsity
- **Authors**: Jean Lucien Randrianantenaina , Hung Nguyen , Nasib Ullah , Jinbin Zhang , Nilesh Gupta , Inderjit S. Dhillon , Rohit Babbar
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41968](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41968)
- **Abstract**:
> Weight pruning can reduce the storage and inference cost of large language models (LLMs), but preserving quality at high sparsity levels remains challenging. Existing methods face a quality-cost trade-off in this regime. One-shot pruning is cheap but degrades sharply as sparsity increases, even when followed by post-pruning recovery. A growing line of work instead optimizes the mask directly from the dense model under the end-to-end objective; among these methods, [Lee et al.,2026], the recent state of the art at extreme sparsity, reaches high quality but at the cost of model-sized optimizer state and auxiliary variables. To address this gap, we propose ETAPS, which prunes gradually and adapts sparsely: it tightens a global sparsity constraint in progressive stages while training a sparse delta with a small, fixed budget. Our key observation is that gradual weight removal keeps each pruning step small enough for sparse adaptation to compensate, allowing the adapted model to provide end-to-end gradients that guide the next pruning decision without model-sized optimizer state. Across six models from 125M to 13B parameters, ETAPS achieves lower perplexity than ELSA at every evaluated sparsity from 50% to 90%, with perplexity reductions of up to 42%. ETAPS is also far cheaper: at 90% sparsity, it prunes LLaMA2-7B on a single H200, where ELSA uses four, with $5.0\times$ smaller total persistent state and 49% fewer GPU-hours. Together, our findings point to a promising direction for achieving high quality in demanding regimes such as extreme sparsity at substantially lower cost.

## 6. ReX-GG: a LLM Ensemble Pipeline for Relation-extraction and Graph Generation
- **Authors**: Giacomo Magnifico , Eduard Barbu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41901](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41901)
- **Abstract**:
> Current LLM ensemble frameworks focus on multi-step setups with additional modules for answer ranking, often opting for token and span analysis rather than structured outputs, leading to heavyweight architectures with potential fail states along the pipeline. Faster, lighter solutions are more vulnerable to hallucination propagation and can lack output control in more complex pipelines. This paper proposes a customisable, lightweight ensemble workflow of coordinated Large Language Models that leverages JSON-structured outputs and anonymous peer-review ranking to mitigate hallucinatory outputs and single-model failure points. The pipeline is demonstrated on a relation extraction task applied to popular science articles in English, targeting four ontologically-grounded relation types (strong causation, weak causation, contrastive, and compositional), with semantic node canonicalisation and interactive, colour-coded HTML causal graphs as the final output. Performance is evaluated through an anonymous user study, achieving an average perceived accuracy of 0.778 against a human-annotated gold standard. The modular architecture supports flexible deployment across both API-based and in-house LLM setups, and the full framework is released under an open license to foster reproducibility and collaborative research.

## 8. Whose Values, Which Contexts, and What Signals? Grounding Cultural Value Alignment with Re3Culture
- **Authors**: Jing Yao , Hyunjin Hwang , Weihua Zheng , Bryan Chen Zhengyu Tan , Jia Wang Peh , Dylan Raharja , JinYeong Bak , Roy Ka-Wei Lee , Rui Cao , Yuki Arase , Jacki O'Neill , Zhicheng Dou , Xing Xie , Xiaoyuan Yi , Furu Wei
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41793](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41793)
- **Abstract**:
> Cultural value alignment requires not only model optimization but also a well-specified target grounded in real-human evidence. Yet how such targets should be grounded in human data remains largely unexplored, raising three fundamental questions: (1) whose values should be represented? (2) where should cultural values be observed? and (3) what signals reveal underlying values? We investigate these questions through Re3Culture, a large-scale dataset linking (i) Representative communities: 700+ human participants across four cultures recruited through cultural-affiliation screening and demographic-aware sampling; (ii) Realistic scenario: culturally sensitive scenarios selected by community members, e.g., family and kinship; and (iii) Revealed value signals: 1,400+ value surveys, 20,000+ topic-grounded behavioral principles and explicit value priorities with responses for 6,000+ situated questions. Our analysis shows why these choices matter for defining the target, with three implications. First, estimating cultural values requires both community affiliation and broad internal coverage. Substituting other cultures or restricting subgroup coverage shifts value estimates. LLM simulations also disproportionately reflect particular subgroups. Second, values must be observed in concrete, community-guided situations. Abstract questionnaire profiles weakly recover situated priorities, and communities differ in which scenarios they identify as culturally value-revealing. Third, value priorities should be elicited explicitly, not learned from response preferences or bottom-line answers alone. Preferences conflate value agreement with other response-level qualities, and similar answers can be derived from different value priorities. By addressing these requirements, Re3Culture provides the first human-grounded resource that explicitly informs target definition for cultural value alignment.

- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41754](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41754)
- **Abstract**:

We propose ERIM, Explicit Rule-Induction for Multimodal In-Context Learning, a unified framework that enhances MICL by explicitly inducing the latent rules governing demonstration samples. - **Abstract**:
> Clinical trial failures are a major barrier to efficient drug development, incurring substantial costs and delaying patient access to effective therapies. ## 11. Breaking Information Islands in Sparse Tuning via Small-World Connectivity
- **Authors**: Xianchao Guan , Zijun Xiong , Yifeng Wang , Yubo Cui , Yaowei Wang , Guo-Sen Xie , Xin Li , Zheng Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41665](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41665)
- **Abstract**:
> Sparse tuning is widely used to adapt large language models due to its parameter efficiency. However, its effectiveness depends not only on the number of trainable parameters, but also on the feature-interaction topology of the sparse update's structural support. Limited or uneven connectivity in this support may isolate subsets of feature dimensions, forming information islands that restrict direct task-specific interactions through the trainable update. To address this issue, we propose Small-World Induced Fine-Tuning (SWIFT), a sparse tuning framework inspired by small-world connectivity. SWIFT combines block-diagonal sparse updates for local feature mixing with a fixed deterministic scattering permutation that routes the mixed outputs across feature groups without additional trainable parameters. We further show theoretically that certain random sparse-update models and block-diagonal updates can exhibit information islands, whereas a sufficiently scattering permutation guarantees positive block-level expansion when the trainable block-diagonal factor consists of equal-sized, structurally dense square blocks. Extensive experiments on commonsense reasoning, natural language generation, and image classification demonstrate that SWIFT outperforms the evaluated parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) baselines on average and can match or surpass full fine-tuning. ## 12. What Should LLMs Forget: Reinforcement Unlearning of Knowledge Associations
- **Authors**: Wutong Xu , Yun Qu , Haiyan Feng , Qi Wang , Xiangyang Ji
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41616](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41616)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) acquire extensive factual knowledge during pre-training, which may include sensitive, private, or copyrighted information, motivating the development of unlearning methods that selectively remove specific knowledge while preserving general capabilities. However, existing approaches typically optimize entire responses without explicitly identifying the knowledge to be forgotten, resulting in coarse and uncontrolled forgetting. To address this, we revisit the forgetting target of LLM unlearning and introduce Knowledge Association (KA), which characterizes the association between a query context and its target knowledge and is instantiated by the corresponding conditional predictive distribution, as the fundamental target of forgetting. Based on KA, we propose Knowledge Association Reinforcement Unlearning (KARU), a unified reinforcement learning framework that suppresses forgotten knowledge associations while preserving retained ones. Specifically, KARU derives complementary mechanisms from the KA-based formulation: token-level reward construction for finer-grained forgetting supervision, distribution-level regularization for precise knowledge suppression, and proximity-aware retain sampling to mitigate collateral degradation. Experiments across diverse LLMs on representative benchmarks demonstrate that KARU consistently improves forgetting effectiveness over existing baselines while maintaining comparable retention performance and general utility.

## 14. Specification-Guided Generation for Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Alessandro Previti , Konstantinos Vandikas , Mario Alviano , Mats Carlsson , Albin Larsson Forsberg , Alexandros Nikou , Athanasios Karapantelakis , Aneta Vulgarakis
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41511)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) can generate structured outputs, but existing constrained-decoding methods mainly enforce syntax and cannot directly enforce satisfaction of a formal specification. We introduce a neuro-symbolic, specification-guided decoding framework that combines LLM generation with symbolic reasoning. At each step, the framework refines the decoding grammar by filtering out values that cannot be extended to an output satisfying the specification. We present a forward-only variant that guarantees specification satisfaction by construction, and a backtracking variant that revises earlier feasible choices when their downstream consequences leave only values that the LLM itself judges unsuitable. The framework is model-agnostic, requires no retraining, and builds on existing grammar-constrained decoding mechanisms. Experiments on configuration and constraint-solving benchmarks show that the forward-only variant reliably produces specification-compliant outputs while reducing repeated LLM inference compared with rejection sampling and incurring modest symbolic-reasoning overhead. The backtracking variant further reduces cases in which the model is forced to select values that it has itself judged unsuitable, thereby substantially improving adherence to the LLM's stated interpretation of the prompt.

## 19. LEAP: Efficient LLM Inference Scheduling with Generalizable Length Prediction
- **Authors**: Kwan Yan , Jialun Zhong , Yanzeng Li
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41359](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41359)
- **Abstract**:
> Length-aware scheduling reduces request latency in LLM serving by prioritizing shorter requests. However, despite their large model sizes, length predictors can suffer degraded ranking performance under domain and task shifts, and adapting them can require costly retraining. Their prediction overhead can further offset the benefits of scheduling. To improve the generalization and efficiency of length-aware scheduling, we propose \textbf{LEAP}, a length prediction system with fast online adaptation and asynchronous CPU execution. ## 20. Do Transaction Prices Reflect Visual Preference? A Diagnostic Study with Multimodal Language Models
- **Authors**: Fengxian Ji , Jingpu Yang , Fan Zhang , Junhong Liang , Qian Jiang , Yuke Li , Jinri Guo , Rongxin Zhu , Zhuohan Xie
