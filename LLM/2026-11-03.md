## 1. From Natural Language to Exact Cover: A Neuro-Symbolic Approach to Zebra Puzzles
- **Authors**: Paulius Skaisgiris , Thomas Pammer , Veronika Semmelrock , Mykyta Ielanskyi , Maximilian Heisinger , Erich Kobler
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03641](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03641)
- **Abstract**:
> Chain-of-Thought (CoT) generation has substantially improved the performance of Large Language Models (LLMs) on complex reasoning tasks, including code generation, data analysis, and exam-style question answering. Despite these advances, purely neural LLMs continue to struggle with elementary logical reasoning problems and lack the determinism, soundness, and reliability characteristic of symbolic reasoning systems. Conversely, classical symbolic methods such as SAT solving and exact cover guarantee correctness and completeness, but require problems to be expressed in highly specialized formal encodings, limiting their applicability to natural language inputs. In this work, we present a synergistic neuro-symbolic framework that bridges this gap by combining neural semantic parsing with deterministic exact cover reasoning. Given a Zebra-style logic puzzle in natural language, an LLM translates entities and constraints into structured tool calls that construct an exact cover instance. A symbolic solver then computes the solution and provides feedback to iteratively refine invalid, under- or overconstrained encodings, exploiting the uniqueness property of Zebra puzzles. We conduct a comprehensive empirical evaluation across multiple parameter scales, post-training paradigms, and LLM families. The results demonstrate that our hybrid approach consistently outperforms strong plain neural baselines, including CoT prompting, as well as recent neuro-symbolic methods.

## 2. DiabAgent: Toward Agentic Physiological Control
- **Authors**: Chengxi Li , Shaoxuan Li , Deyao Hong , Qingle Liu , Yi Huang , Puheng Bian , Minzhi Xu , Lanyue Zhang , Mingze Sun , Xin Li , Calvin Xiao , Shengyuan Wang , Huan-ang Gao , Jiazheng Xu , Kaisen Yang , Hongning Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03601)
- **Abstract**:
> As pretraining scales, medical language models have advanced rapidly in clinical question answering. Their ability to achieve effective physiological control through sustained intervention still lacks systematic quantitative evaluation. Type 1 diabetes provides a concrete setting: impaired insulin secretion creates dependence on exogenous insulin, with dosing continually adjusted using information about meals, exercise, illness, and glucose measurements. We introduce DiabAgent, a research suite for agentic physiological control comprising the Agentic Physiological Control (APC) framework, the DiabetSim simulator, and the SweetBench benchmark. APC controls glucose through medical-device tools, using ongoing observations to infer individual states, accumulate personal experience, and choose treatment actions. We build DiabetSim to simulate different patients, events, and device conditions for scalable virtual testing and training. Using DiabetSim, we construct SweetBench by instantiating 32 scenario designs as 212 glucose-control tasks. With Claude Opus 5.5 and a fixed decision interval of one hour, APC achieves a COGI of 73.5, compared with 71.2 for the open-source closed-loop baseline OpenAPS. Ablations further show that individual adaptation and contextual information improve glucose control. Combining high-frequency rule-based control with lower-frequency agent decisions shows potential for balancing control performance and computational cost. Our work provides a framework, simulator, and evaluation foundation for agents to participate in human physiological control.

## 3. FedReady: Bounded Agents for Federation Formation
- **Authors**: Ziyue Xu , Holger R. Roth , Chester Chen , Peter Cnudde
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03586](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03586)
- **Abstract**:
> Federated learning (FL) runtimes standardize aggregation and deployment, but each new study still requires federation formation: selecting task-eligible sites and preparing their local data and task code for federated training or analysis. This preparation recurs when the target changes, while raw records and site-private mappings are excluded from cross-site FL messages. We present FedReady, a system that performs federation formation with bounded LLM agents. Agents propose shared contracts, local adapters, and task code; the runtime owns workflow state and accepts artifacts only after checks tied to their digests. Candidate site discovery prioritizes recall; local eligibility verification checks task support before site admission. Once participating sites and artifacts are fixed, conventional federated training or analysis proceeds without readiness agents. Across 39 simulated retinal sites and four tasks, discovery reaches recall 1.00 at precision 0.65; local eligibility verification removes all 17 false positives without losing an eligible site-task pair. The participating sites supply 45,575 task-record instances and complete four 100-round jobs, including one with a runtime-generated contract. Across ten patient cohorts, preparation supports seven patient populations and holds endpoints at three sources as unsupported. Comparison with manually prepared reference populations and analysis (the manual reference) gives a maximum survival-curve difference of 0.001133. We also analyze 19 development failures, targeted classification ablations, and standalone and heterogeneous agent configurations. These results establish the feasibility of task-specific data preparation before ordinary FL training or analysis.

## 4. Grading the Unannotated: Insights from the WorkRB Skill Extraction Challenge at RecSys-HR 2026
- **Authors**: Warre Veys , Luis Gasco , Jens-Joris Decorte
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03525)
- **Abstract**:
> Skill extraction benchmarks score models against a few human-annotated positives and treat every other skill in the vocabulary as equally wrong. At the scale of ESCO (13,939 skills) this binary view can reward models that rank the annotated positive high for the wrong reason, and hides semantically nonsensical predictions. The WorkRB Challenge at RecSys-HR 2026 replaces it with graded relevance: every candidate skill in a model's ranking window carries a 0 to 4 grade, and the leaderboard scores nDCG@100 against these grades. Since ternary judgements proved too coarse for objective annotation, relevance is decomposed into a cascade of four binary questions (same domain, activity mentioned, right granularity, replacement of a gold positive) that a smaller LLM judge can answer reliably; the labels are validated with blind human and frontier-model re-annotation. This paper documents the annotation methodology, the construction of five benchmark datasets (four with vacancy sentences as queries, one with lexically distant skill surface forms), the validation of the LLM cascade, and insights from the submissions. The field converged on multi-stage retrieve-and-rerank pipelines, whose gains come from clearing nonsense out of the ranking window rather than finding more positives: at the top of the leaderboard, the graded ordering is uncorrelated with what binary evaluation would have said. Graded labels expose much bigger gaps between top submissions and baselines than binary evaluation does, and show that semantic-understanding skill recommendation is still wide open. The clear next direction is bringing efficiency back: every submitted system we could size uses at least 42x, typically several hundred times, more parameters than the baselines it beats.

## 5. Online Label-Free Gradient Matching for Continual Language Model Training
- **Authors**: Sajjad Pakdamansavoji , Mohammad Reza Rezaei , Ilija Subasic , Pedro Garzon , Radu Paradovschi , Zhao Chen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03496](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03496)
- **Abstract**:
> Language models in customer-facing products must be continually updated, since the data they serve shifts over time. As retraining on the full accumulated data is impractical, each update should instead run on a small set of examples carrying the training signal of all data seen so far. The strongest current methods build this set by gradient matching, which synthesizes examples whose training gradients match those of the full data. However, they require ground-truth labels, run once on a fixed snapshot rather than on a growing stream, and are slow on large data. We introduce \textbf{OLGA}, which lifts these limitations. It distills unlabeled data by drawing the gradient matching targets from the model's own next-token predictions. Across five text classification benchmarks with phi-1.5, it improves accuracy by 10 percentage points over the strongest selection baseline using as few as five examples. OLGA also distills online data streams by keeping a running rehearsal memory of synthetic examples, which is re-distilled together with each new batch as it arrives. In the online setting, it outperforms the strongest selection-based continual baseline by 6.6 stream-AUC percentage points. Finally, our method scales to large data by pairing distillation with selection in a hybrid pipeline, letting selection handle the mild stage of compression and distillation the extreme one. It cuts runtime by 30\% versus distillation alone at similar accuracy, and beats the best selection-only pipeline by up to 12.6 percentage points.

## 7. DeliverBench: Benchmarking AI Agents on Delivering Realistic Multi-Stage Professional Projects

- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03412](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03412)
- **Abstract**:
> LLM agents are judged by what they deliver, and in professional work what gets delivered is a project, not a task. A project pays off only if the whole package holds, and one weak part can limit the value of the rest. Yet existing benchmarks mostly test agents on one task at a time, leaving a gap between the capability we measure and the outcome that makes agents useful. ## 8. Same Outputs, Different Scores: Auditing the Unchecked Graders of Multimodal LLM Benchmarks
- **Authors**: Ahmad Jarjis Hasan , Jonathan Lwowski
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03377)
- **Abstract**:
> Every multimodal-LLM multiple-choice benchmark ends with a scoring rule that converts a model's response, often free-form text rather than a clean option letter, into a right/wrong verdict, buried in its code. Unlike the dataset, this rule is chosen without justification and left unexamined, yet it sets the published number. We reproduce the official scorers of eight peer-reviewed benchmarks on five open vision-language models (VLMs) that fit a single 16 GB GPU, and introduce a blinded human-plus-LLM-judge adjudication (Cohen's kappa=0.956) that recovers what the model actually answered. The scoring rule alone moves accuracy by up to 22 points on identical outputs, yet exactly 0 wherever a model emits clean option letters, so it hides on strong-model leaderboards. It is the fallback and not the rambling that does the damage: the benchmark with the highest non-compliance we measure has an artifact of exactly zero, because its scorer abstains instead of guessing; a reasoning model with non-compliance 0.000 still swings 9.5 points with which regex match of its trace the parser keeps. No standard rule is reliable: snapping a non-answer to its closest option credits answers never given yet buries correct ones, abstention discards answers stated in words, and likelihood scoring follows answer-token priors, so a single grade conflates capability with luck rather than measuring either. We release all code, labels, and the protocol, and argue benchmarks should report a non-compliance rate and at least two rules, not one unexamined grade.

## 9. The SIMPLE Law for Energy Cost of LLM Inference
- **Authors**: Hiari Pizzini Cavagna , Andrea Proia , Daniele Cesarini , Zeynep Kiziltan , Andrea Bartolini
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03330](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03330)
- **Abstract**:
> Generative AI is billed per token, yet tokens are not equally expensive. Each token enters the KV-cache and raises the cost of those that follow, so energy grows non-linearly along a sequence whose tokens look identical. No analytical model states what a token costs, and the most accurate SOTA model is a fit, not a law: it absorbs architecture into fitted coefficients, requiring per-LLM fitting. We derive closed-form expressions of the computational and memory-access complexity of LLM inference in terms of architectural hyperparameters, covering MHA and GQA, and compare a family of analytical models of increasing granularity. From these, we obtain the SIMPLE Inference Model to Predict LLMs Energy (SIMPLE), a four-parameter law that separates prefill from decode, and decode into KV-cache cost and next-token computation. Over traces of 13 dense LLMs from six families on NVIDIA H100 GPUs, it attains R2 = 0.969 and 11.97% MAPE, 3.1-3.5x below the SOTA, without per-LLM calibration. The SIMPLE law explains as well as predicts: prefill energy grows quadratically with the hidden dimension, so larger LLMs suffer most from long inputs; and more aggressive GQA KV grouping reduces the per-token energy by up to 3x relative to standard MHA. Increasing the reasoning token budget pushes output length beyond the analytically derived energy minimum, driving super-linear energy growth; although accuracy grows with the budget, energy grows faster, so Intelligence per Joule peaks at an intermediate budget and falls by up to 3.14x across a 4.0x reasoning-budget range. Agentic serving raises energy per request by 13.0x relative to non-agentic ones, driven by the growth of both the prefill and the KV-cache terms with input length; while prefix caching removes most of the prefill cost (-93%), the KV-cache term is unaffected, so even a cached agentic request costs 4.8x more.

## 10. Toward Knowledge-Centric AI: Discovering, Internalizing, and Using Explicit Knowledge Across Agents and Generations
- **Authors**: Yisen Gao , Haoyu Huang , Zhongwei Xie , Yufei Li , Hong Ting Tsang , Boyi Zhang , Qiao Xiao , Tianshi Zheng , Jiaxin Bai , Yangqiu Song
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03157](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03157)
- **Abstract**:
> Knowledge is how understanding accumulates across people and generations: durable records that outlive their producers and that others can check, combine, teach, and correct. AI agents are now built and run by many independent users and organizations, and each agent is upgraded, replaced, or retired within months. Yet what an agent learns stays trapped in model parameters, a transient context window, or a system-specific memory store that is appended to and retrieved from rather than processed. We argue that the next generation of AI should be knowledge-centric, built around an explicit, continuously evolving knowledge layer independent of any single agent. In this loop, agents discover knowledge from their environments and trajectories; the layer internalizes it by turning fragmented, heterogeneous records and experience into composable, revisable understanding; and agents across systems and generations use the result and return new outcomes for revision. While discovery and use have advanced rapidly with large language model agents, internalization remains the bottleneck. We argue that internalization should become a persistent object of training and maintenance-centered on condition-preserving conceptualization of events and deductive, inductive, and abductive reasoning across noisy, heterogeneous representations (text, tables, graphs, rules, and programs). To power this layer, we call for knowledge foundation models that provide shared neural capacity for knowledge computation, and outline a research agenda showing how an explicit knowledge layer delivers reuse, cost efficiency, auditability, and continuity across agent generations.

## 11. From Local Skills to Long-Horizon Web Tasks: Progressive Skill Exploration for LLM Web Agents
- **Authors**: Xueyang Feng , Xiaohe Bo , Xu Chen , Quanyu Dai , Feng Wen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03116](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03116)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) are driving autonomous agents toward real-world environments, with the Web emerging as one of the most representative interaction scenarios. However, training Web agents with naive exploration remains challenging: successful trajectories are rare, and the sparse terminal rewards make it difficult for agents to obtain useful feedback from failed trajectories. Inspired by skill acquisition theory, we propose Skill-Composed Progressive Exploration (SCOPE), a short-to-long learning framework for long-horizon Web agents that decomposes complex task exploration into a gradual expansion process from local skills to complete task-solving capability. Specifically, SCOPE iteratively explores around local skills: Skill Discovery extracts reusable local skills and failure experiences from historical rollouts; Skill Orchestration composes discovered skills into high-level skill chains; and Skill Expansion leverages failure experiences aligned with the current skill chain to guide the agent toward exploring new subskills. Extensive experiments on WebArena, WebVoyager, and WebShop show that SCOPE consistently improves exploration dynamics and achieves 3.9% to 18.6% relative gains over the strongest baselines in each setting.

## 12. A Fine-Grained Hierarchy and Benchmark for State Tracking in Language Models
- **Authors**: Jackson Petty , William Merrill , Ashish Sabharwal
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03094](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03094)
- **Abstract**:
> Theoretical limitations of transformers on state tracking---the ability to track information about entities or variables over long contexts---have motivated new recurrent language modeling (LM) architectures such as Gated DeltaNet (GDN). These architectures have, in particular, been benchmarked against the $S_5$ word problem, a theoretically hard state-tracking task that captures abilities such as code evaluation under arbitrary variable assignment. It, however, remains open whether other, simpler forms of state tracking might both be more relevant in practice and already explain observed differences between these architectures. We propose evaluating state tracking using a hierarchy of word problems with four increasing levels of difficulty: r-trivial, corresponding to immutable assignment to variables in programming; aperiodic, where assignments are mutable; soluble, where assignments are self-referential; and general finite state tracking. Theoretically, we show that the class of problems transformers are thought to length-generalize on does not even contain all aperiodic state tracking. On the other hand, we prove that GDN with positive eigenvalues expresses all aperiodic problems, and the regular languages recognized by certain transformers are precisely all soluble ones, thus providing a fine-grained picture of state tracking capabilities of various architectures. Taking these ideas to practice, we construct StateBench, a 4-tier long-horizon state-tracking benchmark. Our systematic direct training experiments across architectures largely align with theoretical expectation, albeit with interesting observations at very large scale length-generalization. Additionally, we also evaluate pretrained models---both open hybrid and closed frontier ones---on StateBench, finding that none of them robustly solves even soluble state tracking, suggesting a direction for future improvement.

## 14. PRIME: Predictive Routing of Interventions by Modeling Effects for Scientific Ideation
- **Authors**: Fengxian Ji , Xie Yu , Zhexuan Cui , Yuke Li , Zirui Song , Fan Zhang , Qian Jiang , Jingpu Yang , Xiuying Chen , Preslav Nakov , Zhuohan Xie
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03055](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03055)
- **Abstract**:
> Feedback-driven iteration is widely used to improve LLM-generated scientific ideas, but existing systems either leave revision decisions implicit in generation, align revisions with feedback-localized weaknesses, or restrict action selection to system-specific operations. This is problematic because problems, hypotheses, and solutions are interdependent: the location where a weakness manifests does not itself determine the revision scope that will produce a better successor. We term this phenomenon diagnosis-intervention mismatch. We introduce Predictive Routing of Interventions by Modeling Effects (PRIME), a plug-and-play feedback-to-revision decision layer for existing scientific ideators. PRIME operates in three stages: (1) Structural Attribution interprets feedback over the problem-hypothesis-solution structure without fixing a revision target; (2) Counterfactual Routing constructs alternative scope-conditioned interventions and compares their projected successor utility and risks through lightweight rollouts; and (3) Intervention Execution compiles the selected intervention into an ordered generator-facing plan with semantic actions, preservation constraints, and dependency requirements for successor generation. We validate diagnosis intervention mismatch on 112 human-annotated idea-feedback pairs. Across four benchmarks, two ideators, and fixed- and open-problem settings, PRIME improves successor quality and routing reliability over strong baselines; removing counterfactual routing reduces quality from 3.463 to 1.960 and increases route regret from 0.426 to 1.929, confirming cross-scope effect modeling as the primary source of its gains.

## 15. BLPO: Beyond-Local Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Xuanren Chen , Chongyang Tao , Wenpeng Hu , Yong Wang , Bo Deng , Xinlei Zheng , Shuai Ma
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03005](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03005)
- **Abstract**:
> Long-horizon tasks require large language model (LLM) agents to coordinate interdependent actions, a capability central to automating complex workflows. Stepwise group-based reinforcement learning provides finer credit than trajectory-level supervision, but reliable comparisons must account for differences in interaction history. ## 16. CAREFUL-Bench: Effect-Aware, Judge-Free Evaluation of Multi-Turn LLM Function Calling
- **Authors**: Alexander Kovalenko , Artem Redchych
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02978](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02978)
- **Abstract**:
> The multi-turn checker of the Berkeley Function Calling Leaderboard (BFCL) evaluates final state correctness but ignores the effects of intermediate calls: extra calls are free, function documentation does not describe side effects, and the scripted user never responds to proposals. Consequently, models asking for confirmation before destructive actions are penalized; 260 of 734 reference turns in the base category perform destructive actions without confirmation. We make BFCL effect-aware without replacing it. We infer effect declarations for all 128 functions by executing them in BFCL simulators and comparing state changes, with destructive writes identified by three annotators. A judge-free replay scorer reproduces official results across all 800 reference trajectories while reporting unrequested destructive calls, duplicate writes, redundant reads, surplus steps, and their token costs. We also introduce a confirmation-aware protocol with a deterministic simulated user. Across 11 open models, effect-aware scoring preserves the official ranking but reveals substantial differences in execution quality: the leading model loses 14.0 points due to unrequested destructive calls, while surplus steps consume up to 28% of input tokens. Adding effect hints to function documentation has no impact on eight evaluated models. Allowing the simulated user to respond recovers up to four points for models that seek confirmation. However, the best model completes only six of 576 conversations requiring destructive actions with explicit confirmation. These results expose a key limitation of current function-calling benchmarks: they cannot distinguish between asking for clarification and obtaining permission before potentially destructive actions.

## 17. Action Verification through Conjecture and Refutation for Language Model Agents
- **Authors**: Minhyeon Oh , Chiyeong Heo , Geonwoo Noh , Hoyoung Kim , Jangwon Son , Jungseul Ok
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02950)
- **Abstract**:
> Language model agents increasingly act on users' behalf by manipulating external environments through tools, often from high-level instructions that leave critical task- and environment-specific context unstated. To prevent resulting misaligned actions, recent work verifies proposed actions before execution, largely by establishing whether the conditions supporting them hold. We argue that this confirmation-oriented view leaves a fundamental blind spot: a wrong action often passes every check that asks whether it is right. This motivates a different question for verification: rather than asking what evidence supports an action, can the agent find evidence that refutes it? We pursue this direction with BlackSwan, a refutation-oriented, expect-suspect-test procedure that predicts the intended effect of each state-mutating action, formulates specific and empirically testable hypotheses for how it could be wrong, and tests them using existing observations or additional non-mutating actions, executing the action only if none survives testing. On challenging AppWorld tasks, BlackSwan improves success rate over the strongest baseline by 14.9 and 25.2%p with and without the backbone model's thinking mode, respectively. Even without thinking or in-context demonstrations, it outperforms stronger baselines equipped with them. The gains also transfer beyond AppWorld, with BlackSwan improving WebShop performance by 12.7%p. More broadly, our results suggest that language model agents have an underused capacity to recognize and self-correct misaligned actions, which can be systematically elicited through active conjecture and refutation before execution.

## 18. User Attributes and Political Ideology Shape LLM Receptiveness in Online Political Debates
- **Authors**: Akram Elbouanani , Yousra Taleb , Gianluca Demartini , Adrian Popescu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02908](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02908)
- **Abstract**:
> Online political discussions increasingly involve AI-mediated participation, as users invoke LLMs to assess contested claims, arbitrate disputes, and respond to opposing viewpoints. As these systems become participants in online exchanges, they may alter which speakers and argumentative strategies receive validation within the discussion. We study this interactional dimension by reconstructing 5,376 this http URL debates as LLM conversations and running nearly 150,000 controlled multi-turn conversations that vary user political orientation, expertise, rhetorical strategy, and defended position, including conversations with competing users. Across naturalistic and controlled debates, we find that LLMs are systematically more receptive to left-leaning than right-leaning users. The asymmetry persists when users with different political orientations defend the same position and when opposing users appear in the same conversation, indicating that it is not fully explained by stance agreement or simple accommodation to the current speaker. Repeated interaction increases receptiveness without eliminating this ordering; expertise also matters, while rhetorical differences are smaller. The pattern appears across multiple models, but is not fixed: fine-tuning on different political corpora can largely erase or reverse it. These results show that LLMs can respond systematically differently to participants in the same political debate, making such interactional asymmetries important for understanding their emerging role in online public discourse.

## 19. What makes a theorem interesting? Operationalizing interestingness for open-ended exploration
- **Authors**: Silvère Gangloff
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02905](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02905)
- **Abstract**:
> An agent that explores, whether it generates conjectures or research ideas, must decide which of them are worth keeping. This judgment of interestingness is usually delegated to a hand-picked proxy or to a language model, neither of which is known to correspond to what humans actually value. I study it for mathematical theorems, by observing which results mathematicians single out in formal libraries and in papers. I model each way of singling out results as an evaluator that weights features of a result's statement, its proof and dependencies, and split its weights into a shared and a subjective part. In formalized mathematics, ex-ante features predict these selections as well as a large language-model judge, and the two complement each other. Interestingness is also largely shared. One set of weights, carried mostly by structural features such as the length of the statement and of the proof and the subfields the proof draws on, accounts for most of the signal and transfers in part across formal libraries. What remains subjective depends on practices and individual authors more than on subfields. The judge's advantage lies in its knowledge of the canon, and neither the judge nor the features rely on memory of the papers. The approach, which infers what a community finds interesting from what it singles out, can be applied to other sciences.

## 20. Bridging Low-Level Image Fusion and High-Level Semantic Understanding
- **Authors**: Guangyuan Dong , Lingdong Shen , Xudong Zhang , Haodong Jing , Haoyu Wang , Zihao Li , Zonghao Ying , Ziyu Song , Shenghao Liu , Jiachen Luo , Hao Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02896](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02896)

## 221. C3: Speech-Guided KV Retrieval for Long-Context SpeechLLM Inference
- **Authors**: Dancheng Liu , Markus Müller , Rupak Vignesh Swaminathan , Jing Liu , Srikanth Ronanki , Aram Galstyan
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00333](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00333)
- **Abstract**:
> Speech language models encode audio at a much higher rate than text, causing the KV cache to dominate the memory, bandwidth, and decoding cost of long-form generation. KV retrieval can reduce per-step access by attending to only a query-dependent subset of the cache, but existing methods are designed primarily for text and do not account for the structure of audio attention. We first show that retrieval errors may compound autoregressively into generation drift. We then analyze full audio attention during long-form generation and identify three properties relevant to retrieval: unequal attention concentration across query heads, non-contiguous audio support, and temporal continuity across decoding steps. Guided by these observations, we propose $C^3$(Cluster-Compete-Compete), which organizes audio KVs in a hierarchical key-space index, lets query heads sharing a KV head compete for a common retrieval budget, and adaptively reallocates retrieval resolution through paired split-merge updates. Across long-form speech recognition, translation, and summarization on four SpeechLLMs, $C^3$ achieves the strongest overall performance among retrieval methods under comparable final-KV access budgets, with particularly large gains in the tight-budget regime, while improving long-context decoding efficiency and accelerator-memory scaling.

## 223. Kerkoporta: Injecting Structured-Output Backdoors in Reasoning Coding Agents
- **Authors**: Georgios Syros , Blake Bullwinkel , Alina Oprea , Cristina Nita-Rotaru , Amanda Minnich , Xiaodong Liu , Giorgio Severi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.00138)
- **Abstract**:
> Existing large language model (LLM) backdoor attacks operate primarily in chat settings, where the risk is bounded by free-form text outputs. AI agents remove that bound by performing actions and executing commands on the user's machine. We introduce structured-output backdoor attacks against reasoning models in agentic coding settings. A trigger placed anywhere in the agent's context causes the model to emit an attacker-chosen tool call, which the harness executes, directly compromising the system's confidentiality, integrity, or availability. We present Kerkoporta, a methodology that constructs high-quality poison samples, injects the backdoor via supervised fine-tuning, and recovers utility loss with lightweight reinforcement learning. We evaluate on modern, heavily post-trained open-weight models across proprietary and open-source harnesses, focusing on coding for its wide adoption and long-horizon task structure. The attack reaches success rates of up to 96.2% and can be configured to generalize across harnesses or bind to a single target. The resulting models can outperform the clean baseline on standard utility benchmarks, giving victims a direct incentive to adopt them. Overall, we demonstrate that LLM backdoors can have critical security impacts in the context of modern coding agents, and we characterize the poisoning methodology required to insert backdoors that are both effective and stealthy in this setting.
