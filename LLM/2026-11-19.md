## 1. Depth Before Breadth: Reverse-to-Forward Curriculum with Advantage Decomposition for LLM Agent
- **Authors**: Siyuan Du , Yuxin Liu , Meng Tang , Haoran Li , Yechao Bai , Feiyang Pan , Jia He
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24697](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24697)
- **Abstract**:
> Reinforcement learning (RL) has become a common approach for training large language model (LLM) agents on long-horizon tasks. However, learning from sparse terminal rewards makes credit assignment and exploration difficult. We approach these challenges through a policy-induced capability region, the set of states from which the current policy can reliably complete a task. Near its boundary, an agent may need only a few actions to reach a more solvable state, even when the goal remains distant. This reduces long-horizon learning to two coupled subproblems of reaching that state and completing the task from there. We address them with paired advantages and a progressive reverse-to-forward curriculum. The advantages provide separate signals for reaching more solvable states and completing the task from them, while the curriculum expands the capability region backward along successful reference trajectories and then into off-reference states through on-policy exploration. On ALFWorld and WebShop, our method outperforms competitive agentic RL methods at both model scales, achieving success rates of 90.1%/96.1% and 80.2%/82.6% with Qwen2.5-1.5B/7B-Instruct, respectively.

## 3. GateWatch: Low-Overhead LLM Monitoring with Boolean Circuits
- **Authors**: Firas Spanioli , Yaniv David
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24619](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24619)
- **Abstract**:
> As large language models (LLMs) and LLM-based agents are increasingly deployed, monitoring their behavior becomes important for detecting unsafe or unintended actions. Activation probes offer an efficient approach, but existing methods face trade-offs between accuracy, interpretability, and computational overhead. Probes on raw activations lack interpretable inputs, whereas sparse autoencoder (SAE) probes use interpretable features but often rely on additive classifiers and large SAE encoders, limiting interpretability and increasing memory overhead. We introduce GateWatch, a compact, interpretable classifier that expresses its entire decision rule as an explicit Boolean logic over a small number of binarized SAE features. We explore three approaches to constructing these circuits under a specified gate budget. For efficient deployment, GateWatchServe computes only selected SAE features, enables streaming of their binary values across layers during inference, and evaluates multiple GateWatch classifiers in parallel using compact lookup tables. Across 24 tasks on Gemma-2-9B, GateWatch achieves 80.49% mean test accuracy using only 3.8 SAE features and 3.5 logic gates per probe on average, just 1.17 percentage points below full 16K-feature SAE probes, while matching or outperforming them on 10 tasks and reducing probe memory by 99.98%. Inside vLLM, 1,000 concurrent multi-layer, multi-token GateWatch classifiers retain 97-98% of baseline throughput and 99.8% of KV-cache capacity.

## 4. NaviGATE: Noise-Aware Dual-View Generation for Multimodal Knowledge Graph Reasoning
- **Authors**: Jie Peng , Yongxue Shan , Yongfu Zha , Cui Miao , Xiaodong Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24552)
- **Abstract**:
> Multimodal knowledge graph reasoning (MKGR) aims to infer missing facts by jointly exploiting relational structures, textual descriptions, and visual evidence. Existing embedding-based methods effectively capture graph topology, but their fixed multimodal representations are less effective for query-specific fine-grained semantic reasoning, while generation-based methods are vulnerable to noisy retrieval and may produce invalid entities. To address these challenges, we propose NaviGATE, a noise-aware generative framework for MKGR. NaviGATE first aligns graph-derived structural representations with textual semantics, then performs structure-aware internal knowledge activation by fine-tuning the multimodal language model with multimodal entity summaries and injected structural tokens. In the noise-aware dual-view instruction tuning stage, NaviGATE jointly optimizes retrieval-free and retrieval-aware views: reliable retrieved candidates are reinforced through contrastive logit sharpening, while misleading candidates are suppressed through parametric consistency distillation. In inference, structure-constrained decoding restricts generation to valid entities in the target knowledge graph. Experiments on standard benchmarks show that NaviGATE consistently outperforms strong unimodal, multimodal, and generation-based baselines, and remains robust under noisy retrieval scenarios.

## 5. ABSClaw: From Invented Worlds to Reality-Grounded Social Simulation
- **Authors**: Shaobin He , Yan Gao , Quan Zheng , Zhenyang Lin , Zhen Liu , Jiyan He , Shuxin Zheng
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24517](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24517)
- **Abstract**:

In this paper, we introduce ABSClaw, a zero-code agent that compiles natural-language requests into executable, reality-grounded social simulations. Furthermore, we propose ABSBench, a benchmark of 193 real-world social-simulation tasks. On ABSBench, ABSClaw achieves the best overall performance among the evaluated social simulators. Three real-world case studies show that grounding personas, environments, and interaction networks each improves agreement with observed outcomes. With one runtime skill, ABSClaw executes 13 population-scale simulations, collectively involving 25 million agents. Together, these results demonstrate ABSClaw's real-world fidelity, extensibility, and scalability.

## 6. Resolution as Attention for Efficient Agent Memory Perception
- **Authors**: Siqi Xiang , Zhipeng Xu , Shengrui Liu , Zulong Chen , Chunyan Miao , Shijian Lu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24422](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24422)
- **Abstract**:
> LLM-based agents accumulate extensive interaction histories when solving complex, long-horizon tasks, making efficient memory management critical to their performance and scalability. While existing memory management methods primarily focus on what to retain or retrieve, they often overlook how much perceptual detail should be allocated to individual past interactions. We posit that effective agent memory depends not only on the information preserved, but also on the granularity at which each past interaction is perceived. Motivated by this insight, we propose Resolution as Attention (RAA), a memory perception mechanism that uses visual resolution as a means of attention allocation across interaction histories. RAA renders past interactions as images and dynamically adjusts their resolution at every step according to their relevance to the agent's upcoming decision, preserving fine-grained information for critical interactions while reducing visual-token consumption for less relevant ones. To adaptively determine the perceptual fidelity of each interaction, we train a lightweight relevance estimator with teacher-model guidance and further optimize it to preserve the agent's decision consistency at a reduced visual-token cost. Experiments across multiple tasks and backbones show that RAA reduces token consumption by over 50% without compromising task performance. Moreover, RAA can be integrated as a plug-in perception layer with existing memory management methods, yielding further token savings and revealing adaptive perception as a complementary dimension of agent memory.

## 8. MoE-Context: Show the Model Only What It Needs
- **Authors**: Vedant Navale
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24379)
- **Abstract**:
> AI agents read their whole history at every step. People do not work like that: we keep what the moment needs and hold the rest as a rough note we can recall. MoE-Context applies this to language models. The context is split into blocks. Before each step a fast yes or no pass decides which blocks the step needs; the rest stay as one-line notes that can be brought back, and nothing is deleted. In the version we build, one model makes this decision and then answers, over a single key-value cache where hidden blocks are masked rather than removed. On a laptop with Qwen3-1.7B and a synthetic lookup task, showing only the needed block raises accuracy from 93 to 119 of 120, and a masked block is as hidden as a deleted one. The model's own picking is the weak part. A supervised linear probe written into spare output rows lifts its accuracy from 80 to 110 of 120, but simple keyword search picks perfectly on this task, and the probe does not transfer to a second task. We report these results openly, along with two cheap usage patterns that block switching enables: undoing a failed attempt and running subagents without a new context.

## 9. Semantic Context Handoff with On-Demand Evidence for Long-Horizon Agents
- **Authors**: Guolong Zheng , Chen Hou , Xu Yang , Jianshan Zhang , Tao Huang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24357)
- **Abstract**:
> Traditional long-horizon LLM agents often hand off context through full-history prompting, retaining all previous action--observation interactions. As execution proceeds, this history keeps growing, increasing inference cost and potentially degrading accuracy. Existing methods control this growth by preserving compact conclusions while removing earlier observations, but doing so can discard evidence that later becomes relevant. We introduce Semantic Context Handoff (SCH), whose central abstraction is state--evidence separation: a compact semantic state carries the agent's current interpretation, while supporting action--observation evidence remains separately addressable and can re-enter context when later execution makes it relevant, without separate summarization or retrieval-model calls. Empirically, SCH improves long-horizon agent performance while substantially reducing context cost. In a controlled 150-task SWE-bench study, SCH's evidence recovery improves Pass@1 from $80.0\%$ to $86.9\%$ at essentially unchanged token use and execution time. In the complete-system comparison, SCH achieves 86.9% Pass@1 while using 43% fewer tokens and 41% less wall-clock time than the strongest baseline by task performance. Across models and benchmarks, SCH consistently reduces token use relative to full-history prompting while remaining competitive with or improving upon the evaluated baselines. Trajectory analysis confirms that SCH recovers evidence from substantially earlier parts of the interaction history rather than merely preserving a recent context window.

- **Authors**: Boxi Zhou , German Sviridov , Dmitrii Ustiugov , Haris Javaid
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24324](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24324)
- **Abstract**:
> AI coding agents have become one of the most popular applications of large language models. Yet, agentic AI comes at the cost of a tremendous amount of GPU compute resources, which limits its profitability and its viability at scale. In this paper, we investigate whether smaller, cheaper models can cut this cost without sacrificing accuracy. We evaluate 36 popular large and small open-weight models released since 2024 on a software engineering benchmark, and find that recent small models are closing the accuracy gap compared to their large counterparts. ## 16. Test-Time Symmetrization: Scaling Laws for Model-Agnostic Learning under Structure
- **Authors**: Behrooz Tahmasebi , Melanie Weber
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24032](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24032)
- **Abstract**:
> Test-time compute has emerged as a powerful way to improve the accuracy of language models at inference time, with scaling laws quantifying how accuracy improves with additional forward passes. Existing scaling laws, however, focus on repeated sampling from a fixed prompt, even though many tasks admit multiple equivalent representations of the same underlying problem. A graph does not depend on how its nodes are numbered, and a program does not depend on the names of its variables, yet language models can change their answers under such rewritings. In this paper, we study test-time symmetrization, which uses forward passes on rewritings of the input and aggregates the resulting answers over the group $G$ of rewritings. We prove that no amount of sampling and repeated decoding from a single representation can replace rewriting, while only $O(\log |G|)$ random rewritings suffice to recover the information provided by all $|G|$ rewritings. The same set of $O(\log |G|)$ rewritings provides simultaneous approximation across models, decoding rules, and sampled reasoning traces, and the logarithmic scaling is optimal in general. We validate the theory with experiments on Qwen2.5-7B, Phi-4-mini, Llama-3.1-8B, and Qwen2.5-14B, with chain-of-thought reasoning, on graph reasoning, logical deduction, and multiple-choice question answering. At the same number of forward passes, spending them on rewritings of the input is substantially more accurate than spending them on repeated sampling from the original prompt whenever the model's answer depends on how the input is written, and the number of rewritings needed grows only logarithmically with the number of possible rewritings, as the theory predicts. Our results provide a principled way to enforce symmetries at inference time when they cannot be built into the model, which we call model-agnostic symmetrization.

## 17. SCAFFOLD: Toward Warrant-Preserving Evidence Transport for Reusable LLM-Agent Skills

## 21. A System 1 Decision Model for Medical Multiple-Choice Questions Built on Frozen Language Models
- **Authors**: Pavan Kumar Mantha
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23832)
- **Abstract**:
> A System 1 model answers a multiple-choice question in a single pass.
