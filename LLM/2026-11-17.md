## 1. Can Mechanistic Interpretability Help Explain LLM Agent Failures? A Case Study on Web Element Hallucination
- **Authors**: Mohamed Aghzal , Mor Geva , Gregory J. Stein , Ziyu Yao
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18514](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18514)
- **Abstract**:
> Mechanistic Interpretability (MI) has made significant progress in explaining the internal computations of Large Language Models (LLMs); however, these advances have yet to demonstrate comparable success in explaining the decision-making processes of LLM-based agents. This paper examines the challenges MI tools face when applied to realistic agentic failures. We focus on one such failure: web agents hallucinating actions that are not available on a website. We apply a series of mechanistic analyzes to Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as it interacts with live websites. We find that a linear probe trained on the last prompt token can predict hallucinations before generation begins (0.80 AUC) and steering along a probe direction corrects 85\% of hallucinations, but disrupts 7\% of valid actions. However, these results only provide a partial explanation of why the agent hallucinates. The probe direction is sensitive to prompt formatting, losing much of its steering effect after a minor change to how actions are represented. Additionally, circuit identification can be difficult as the computation is distributed across multiple components and positions, and the information the agent relies on varies across examples. We discuss how these observations challenge the assumptions underlying standard MI methods for circuit discovery and feature identification, and outline future directions for making these methods better suited to explain failures in LLM-based agents.

## 2. ARCHER: From Rationale Imitation to Executable Reasoning in Small Language Models
- **Authors**: Dhruv Dixit , Khushi Patel , Congcong Xu , Min Song
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18494)
- **Abstract**:
> Small language models are attractive for local, low-cost, and privacy-sensitive deployment, but they remain unreliable on mathematical reasoning tasks that require decomposition, exact computation, tool use, and verification. We introduce \textbf{ARCHER}: \textbf{A}ction-trace \textbf{R}einforcement for \textbf{C}alculator-grounded \textbf{H}ierarchical \textbf{E}xecutable \textbf{R}easoning, a two-stage framework for training small language models (SLMs) to produce executable and auditable reasoning trajectories. In the first stage, ARCHER compiles free-form chain-of-thought rationales into typed action traces containing reasoning steps, calculator calls, deterministic runtime observations, verification actions, and final answers. This shifts the supervision target from imitating explanatory text to learning a tool-grounded reasoning policy. In the second stage, ARCHER refines this policy through asymmetric hierarchical reinforcement learning, using grouped and conditionally gated trajectory rewards for answer correctness, tool grounding, process discipline, and trace alignment. Across five standard sub-10B backbones, ARCHER improves over the strongest tool-augmented baseline by 5.51 percentage points on macro-average across benchmark accuracies, including gains of 8.95 points on MATH-500 and 5.33 and 5.34 points on AIME-2024 and AIME-2025. Evaluations on two additional reasoning-specialized 7B backbones further show that executable grounding remains beneficial after reasoning-focused reinforcement learning or distillation. These results demonstrate that executable supervision and reward-guided behavioral refinement provide a general and effective training paradigm for compact mathematical reasoners.

## 3. Typing Matters: Muscle, Language and Typing Behaviour in Live Keystroke Decoding from sEMG
- **Authors**: Abhilash Durgam , Nyle Siddiqui , Mubarak Shah , Qiushi Fu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18464](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18464)
- **Abstract**:
> Surface electromyography (sEMG) from the wrists can recover typed text without a keyboard. It is usually posed as a signal-inference problem: characters are read out of the muscle signal and character error rate is reported offline. We argue that this framing removes most of the problem. The decoder is constrained simultaneously by the muscles, by the language being typed, and by typing behaviour such as typos, corrections, backspaces and line breaks, and a usable system must predict keys live while the hand keeps moving. Existing systems fall short on two counts: (1) they process sEMG in fixed windows and carry nothing from one window to the next, discarding context accumulated over a session, and (2) they lack any understanding of the linguistic structure that drives typing, so every keystroke is inferred from the muscle signal alone. We propose a fully causal, zero-lookahead sEMG typing decoder built on a state space model (SSM), in which a single model decodes a whole session without windowed partitions, conditioned on what has already been typed. We achieve this by fusing a traditional typing decoder with an efficient autoregressive language model, instilling both sEMG and linguistic understanding in one model. To train such a model for per-character typing rather than whole-word text, we introduce a retokenization method that rewrites ordinary prose as human-like typing, with keystrokes, typos, backspaces and line breaks included, converting WikiText into a large typing corpus where none existed. Our model, having never seen a single sEMG-to-character transcript in typing, achieves a state-of-the-art character error rate of 25.13% on emg2qwerty's cross-user benchmark, a 5.4% relative improvement over the previous best with 6.7 times fewer parameters, while running strictly causally with no future context.

## 4. LECAT: Measuring LLM Category Learning from Pretraining Data
- **Authors**: Jimin Mun , Maarten Sap , Yanai Elazar
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18419](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18419)
- **Abstract**:
> Humans know that airplanes are fast, that this extends to red airplanes, but that it does not hold for broken airplanes. How do LLMs acquire category knowledge that humans learn and apply effortlessly? We introduce LeCat, a two-part framework to analyze LLM's Learning of Category knowledge. First, we use LeCat to evaluate models on their ability to learn category knowledge, distinguishing between three cases: memorization of in-domain category knowledge, generalization to unseen instances, and ability to flexibly use knowledge in appropriate contexts. Then, we use LeCat to trace how this knowledge can be learned in pretraining from text that may encode category knowledge in various ways, such as verbatim repetition or exceptions, which we refer to as forms of "knowledge expression". We first assess open-source pretrained models using 199 curated category-feature pairs and analyze their training data. Our findings suggest that strengthening context and category-feature co-occurrence likelihood (PMI) improve learning for larger models and verbatim rule-like statements diminish flexible category learning. To systematically isolate these effects, we then train a 68M model from scratch with precise control over the categorical information each sees in their training data, confirming that verbatim repetition reduces flexible learning but mixture of diverse knowledge expressions lead to more robust category learning. Together, our findings highlight that while contextual flexibility is persistently difficult for models, how category knowledge is expressed in pretraining greatly impacts models' acquisition of robust category knowledge.

## 5. VelServe: Taming Decode Jitters in LLM Inference with Tail-Calibrated Scheduling
- **Authors**: Ishaan Datta , Alind Khare , Koushik Kumar Dey , Ratnesh Jamidar , Anand Kumar Singh
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18392](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18392)
- **Abstract**:
> Interactive LLM services must meet latency service-level objectives (SLOs) for the time to first token (TTFT) and the time between tokens (TBT), as streaming and soft real-time applications require predictable token delivery. We identify decode jitters in both aggregated and disaggregated serving. A jitter delays every request in the batch, and long responses span hundreds of steps, so even rare overruns cause many SLO violations. In aggregated serving, SLO-aware schedulers set each step's prefill budget until its predicted latency just meets the TBT target, leaving no room for mispredictions or host stalls. Disaggregation meets average TBT targets at a cost in capacity, yet the steps that admit new requests still run late. We present \sysname, a scheduler that calibrates how much prefill work each decode step carries using the measured tail of its own iteration latency. The SLO determines how rare a late step must be, and \sysname leaves enough headroom in each iteration to prevent overruns and meet the target decode latency. This headroom slows prefill, so \sysname serves the shortest waiting prompt first. When TTFT deadlines grow with prompt length, the long prompts it delays have the most time to spare. Under disaggregation, it spaces admissions so that no request waits on more slow admission steps than its P99 target allows. On three attention families and varying workloads, \sysname sustains 2.4--2.9$\times$ the SLO goodput of the strongest baseline on Qwen3-8B and DeepSeek-V4-Flash, and at least 4.4$\times$ on DeepSeek-V2-Lite. With prefill and decode disaggregated on eight GPUs, \sysname meets the SLO for more than twice as many requests as state-of-the-art baselines.

## 7. Experts Across Schemas: Agent-Guided Mixture-of-Relational-Experts for Cross-Database Generalization
- **Authors**: Hiren Madhu , Sukanta Ganguly , Kiran Srinivasan , Smita Krishnaswamy , Rex Ying
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18315](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18315)
- **Abstract**:
> Web platforms record user and item activity in relational databases whose schemas differ from platform to platform, which makes it difficult to transfer predictive models between them. Relational foundation models address this through cross-database pretraining, yet recent improvements have relied on increasingly large pretraining corpora. We ask whether a better architecture, together with features specific to the target database, can deliver comparable gains from far less pretraining data. We introduce MoREGT, a mixture-of-relational-experts graph transformer that represents both rows and cells as tokens, adds the log-ages of rows and the names of foreign-key relationships to attention, and routes cells and rows to experts by column metadata rather than by values. The agent's columns raise mean AUROC from 78.45 to 79.63 and lower mean nMAE from 27.91 to 26.98, the highest means on both metrics among the compared methods, including RT-J, which pretrains on about 400 times as many databases.

## 8. Dancing Tokens on the Vocabulary Network from LLM Generation
- **Authors**: Yuan-Chao Hu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18248](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18248)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) generate text one token at a time, yet how a particular generation relates to the geometry learned in pre-training remains unclear. The fundamental explainability of LLMs is elusive. Here, we build a similarity network from the input embeddings of six open LLMs and map generation onto it. The token-embedding network is scale-free while heavy-tailed, as are random embeddings with matched covariance, but far more fragmented. Distillation barely changes this topology because input embeddings are largely inherited. The knowledge space is thus deterministic from pre-training. Consecutive generated tokens are rarely graph neighbours and, in most models, no more often than in shuffled order, so generation is not a walk on the predefined network, although the tokens it visits are concentrated within it. Through the logit lens in a layer- wise manner, tokens settle late in depth in most models, and repetitive models remain confined to small domain-specific sub-graphs. These observables could serve as inference-time monitors of degeneration. We thus provide a fresh perspective on understanding LLM generation in the microscopic way from node or link prediction by graph learning.

## 9. Large Language Model-Empowered Intelligent Agent for Adaptive Fast-Charging Control of Lithium-Ion Batteries
- **Authors**: Joey Chan , Haoyu Pan , Zhen Chen , ErShun Pan
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18140](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18140)
- **Abstract**:
> Fast charging of lithium-ion batteries is a multi-objective control problem that requires reducing charging time and sustaining high energy throughput while suppressing accelerated degradation and safety-critical electrochemical reactions. Conventional protocols are easy to implement but have limited ability to adapt to variations in battery chemistry, operating conditions, and aging state. Model-based optimization and conventional data-driven control often require accurate models, costly iterative search, or task-specific policies. We develop a large language model (LLM)-empowered intelligent agent for adaptive battery fast-charging control. In this closed-loop framework, the agent interprets structured battery states, generates executable charging actions, observes the resulting battery response, and continuously adjusts subsequent decisions. Our two-stage strategy combines supervised fine-tuning (SFT) and Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). SFT establishes battery-control semantics and feasible decision priors from simulation-derived demonstrations. GRPO aligns the agent with a multi-objective control objective covering energy throughput, degradation mitigation, and operational constraints. Closed-loop simulations show that, under matched energy-throughput requirements, the agent achieves longer cycling life than conventional charging baselines and optimization-based strategies across heterogeneous battery configurations. This study provides a simulation-grounded proof of concept for using LLM agents as sequential decision makers in battery management and takes a step toward language-model-enabled control of physical energy systems.

## 11. LACCO: Local Accumulation with Computation--Communication Overlap
- **Authors**: Daniele Kopyshevskiy (MLIA), Benjamin Th{é}rien (Mila, UdeM), Nicolas Pinto , Edouard Oyallon (MLIA)
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17991](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17991)
- **Abstract**:
> Training language models across low-bandwidth networks requires reducing synchronization overhead. Local optimization methods such as DiLoCo communicate only after several local steps, but outer gradient averaging and the outer update remain on the critical path. Existing computation-communication overlap methods avoid this stall through delayed or replica-local updates, which can degrade convergence or cause replicas to drift. We propose LACCO (Local Accumulation with Computation-Communication Overlap), a two-stage algorithm that overlaps global communication with local computation while applying globally aggregated updates shared by every replica, without introducing staleness in the outer update. These gains remain robust as model size scales up to 0.5B parameters and across a range of communication intervals and numbers of replicas up to 16 workers, while incurring only negligible-or fully overlappable-computational or memory overhead, which demonstrates the strength of LACCO.

## 16. Soteria: Formally Verified Planning with Runtime Enforcement for Safe LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Mike He , Ankush Desai , Sharad Malik , Aarti Gupta
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17880](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17880)
- **Abstract**:
> LLM agents that execute multi-step tool calls must satisfy two objectives simultaneously: completing the user's task (utility) and conforming to domain policies and correctness constraints (safety). These objectives are in tension -- blocking an unsafe action prevents a violation but can leave the agent stranded with no principled way to recover. Existing approaches sacrifice one objective for the other. We introduce Soteria, a framework that reconciles safety and utility through verified hierarchical planning and runtime enforcement of specifications. Before execution, the agent generates a structured plan that is formally verified against the specifications prior to any tool invocation. During execution, the verified plan serves dual roles: it ensures that the agent takes specification-conformant trajectories and provides guidance when unsafe actions are blocked. Across multiple benchmarks covering a diverse range of tool-use tasks, Soteria achieves 1.7 times the average task success rate of the best baselines, with no violations of policy specifications, demonstrating that safety and utility need not be traded off.

## 18. DECODE: Diagnosing Interpretability Failures in Automated Interpretation Process
