## 1. AI-Driven Reproducibility and Analytical Robustness in Scientific Discovery
- **Authors**: Shicheng Liu , Krista Opsahl-Ong , Andrew Drozdov , Julian Killingback , Arnav Singhvi , Ronak Pradeep , Michael Bendersky , James C. Dickerson , Monica S. Lam
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23845)
- **Abstract**:
> Reproducible and analytically robust research findings are essential to scientific progress, but evaluating these properties typically requires costly manual effort. We investigate whether LLM agents can help automate reproduction and robustness analysis, using a medical case study grounded in the Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results (SEER) database, a widely used U.S. cancer registry underlying tens of thousands of published studies. First, we introduce SEER-Repro, a benchmark of 20 published SEER-based papers together with the data and resources required to reproduce their findings. Second, we create an AI agent that successfully reproduces 77.4% of 2,190 atomic claims, while providing executable baselines for further analysis. Third, we develop an agentic analysis pipeline to test the robustness of the medical findings by identifying analytical choices underlying each finding, proposing defensible alternatives, and rerunning the analysis to determine whether those choices affect the conclusion. Across published papers in SEER-Repro, our pipeline identifies potentially consequential analytical choices in 9.5% of fully reproduced findings and flags them for expert review. These results show how LLM agents can support scientific review by reproducing findings, systematically testing their robustness, and directing expert attention toward potentially consequential analytical choices.

## 2. Beyond Large Language Models: A Mental World Model Framework for Mental Modeling, Simulation, Evolution, and Interpretability
- **Authors**: Qingshi Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23722](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23722)
- **Abstract**:
> Understanding and simulating how subjective cognition and mental states emerge, evolve, and shape behavior is essential for advancing artificial intelligence toward deeper social cognition and general intelligence. Existing large language models (LLMs) and Theory of Mind (ToM) methods primarily infer mental states from linguistic information, lacking unified mechanisms for subjective world construction, cognitive-psychological interactions, dynamic evolution, and historical dependencies. This paper introduces the Mental World Model (MWM) and its implementation, the Multi-Branch Mental Evolution Architecture (MMEA), which explicitly constructs subjective worlds, models interactions between cognition and mental states, and simulates continuous mental evolution shaped by historical experiences. On DynToM and RecToM, MMEA achieves 96.48% and 94.00% accuracy, respectively, outperforming leading LLMs and specialized ToM methods. Additional experiments demonstrate improvements of 28.7, 39.3, and 16.6 percentage points in mental state and behavior prediction, long-sequence prediction, and implicit mental state inference, respectively. The results demonstrate that MMEA provides an interpretable framework for continuous mental modeling, enabling AI to move beyond surface-level semantic representations to infer individuals' underlying mental states from linguistic information and dynamically simulate their internal cognitive processes and evolution over time. MWM also provides a promising foundation for causal reasoning, personalized AI, sustained human-AI interaction, and future advances toward artificial general intelligence (AGI).

## 3. From Trajectories to Differentiated Skills: Cognitive Differentiation for Self-Improving Agents
- **Authors**: Yewei Zhao , Derong Xu , Wei Chen , Yin Xu , Peilun Zhou , Xiaohan Wang , Guojun Yin , Wei Lin , Mingjun Xiao
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23599](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23599)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) agents tackle complex tasks through reasoning and interaction with external environments. Agent skills provide persistent, transferable capabilities that guide task planning, inform contextual decisions, support tool use, and enable executable procedures. Yet existing skill-evolution methods largely organize knowledge around skill artifacts, overlooking the functional heterogeneity within individual skills and its implications for knowledge maintenance and use. Inspired by cognitive science, we propose Cognitive Differentiation in Skill Evolution (CDSE), which evolves a unified skill through four functionally distinct yet coordinated components: Meta, Experience, Tool, and Script. CDSE extracts evidence-grounded semantic gradients from successful and failed trajectories, consolidates them by functional role, and applies component-specific updates and validation with cross-component coordination and adaptive invocation. An analysis of 500 top-ranked skill packages shows that 96.8% contain knowledge spanning at least two functional roles. Across six benchmarks, three agent models, and two execution harnesses, CDSE achieves the highest scores in all 36 settings, improving over the Base agent by 7.6 percentage points and the strongest competing method in each setting by 2.6 points on average. Ablation studies further support the importance of functional differentiation and adaptive invocation.

## 4. RL-Guided Temporal Localization for Dual-Channel Retrieval in Long-Horizon Agent Memory
- **Authors**: Dabin Sheng , Zhe Wu , Jinming Zhao , Junliang Xing , Yuanchun Shi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23598](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23598)
- **Abstract**:
> Time-aware retrieval is crucial for long-horizon LLM agents to effectively leverage ever-growing long-term memory. Existing methods focus on lexical or semantic similarity, leading to target memories being crowded out by temporally mismatched yet semantically similar candidates. Even time-aware approaches inject temporal cues only during memory organization or answer generation, overlooking temporal constraints at the retrieval stage, where candidate selection occurs - the root cause of the displacement. Inspired by the temporal contiguity effect in human episodic memory, we propose TIDER (Temporal Interval-driven Dual-channel Evidence Retrieval), a time-aware agentic retrieval framework. Central to TIDER is a reinforcement learning (RL)-trained temporal interval locator that infers the temporal windows in which the evidence lies. RL optimizes window localization as a discrete, non-differentiable retrieval decision, using composite rewards to convert sparse answer supervision into fine-grained retrieval feedback. These temporal windows first define the temporal retrieval channel; the retrieved candidates are then merged with results from the semantic channel and reranked by proximity to the windows. This temporal grounding enables TIDER to retrieve temporally aligned evidence that would otherwise be drowned out by the volume of semantically similar yet temporally mismatched memories. With a lightweight 1.7B locator, TIDER achieves state-of-the-art accuracy on both long-horizon memory benchmarks - 61.52% on LifeBench (+8.82%) and 78.96% on LoCoMo (+3.66%) - which indicates that temporal cues are crucial for pinpointing target evidence amid a vast pool of semantically similar historical events in long-term daily-life memory.

## 5. Darwin-Claw: Environment-Adaptive Experience Consolidation for Recursively Self-Evolving Agents
- **Authors**: Mingze Yin , Xiaohan Wang , Dian Li , Longfei Luo , Jiahong Yan , Yiheng Zhu , Chang-Yu Hsieh
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23574)
- **Abstract**:
> While Large Language Model (LLM) agents have demonstrated remarkable capabilities across complex tasks, they predominantly operate in isolated sessions, lacking the ability to continually learn from historical interactions or adapt to user-specific environments. Existing self-evolution paradigms often indiscriminately inject raw interaction trajectories into memory or skill modules, leading to obscured behavioral patterns and compromised update controllability. Furthermore, as foundational LLM reasoning improves, the bottleneck for agentic evolution shifts from accumulating generic knowledge to effectively managing situational experience. To address this, we propose Darwin-Claw, an environment-adaptive self-evolving framework that systematically distills episodic experiences to drive the comprehensive evolution of operational skills and durable memory. Theoretically grounded in an iterative agent-actor and evolver-critic co-adaptation, Darwin-Claw introduces a Hierarchical Experience Consolidation System that extracts fine-grained error attributions and generalizes them into dynamically updated prototype vectors. This structured situational experience serves as interaction patches for context-sensitive perception and explicit optimization signals for skill refinement. Practically, Darwin-Claw is a readily deployable, automated framework compatible with mainstream agent systems without requiring manual curation. Extensive experiments on WildClawBench demonstrate that Darwin-Claw yields substantial performance improvements, establishing a controllable and stable pathway for the continual, user-centric adaptation of autonomous agents in real-world ecosystems.

## 6. SkillBrowser: Selective Skill Exposure for Scalable Skill-Using Agents
- **Authors**: Jin Ye , Yifan Jiang , Lan-zhe Guo
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23554](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23554)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model agents increasingly rely on skills to extend their capabilities in complex tasks. However, scalable skill use requires Selective Skill Exposure (SSE): providing relevant procedural content when needed while avoiding unnecessary exposure that may disrupt reasoning. Existing paradigms fall short in complementary ways. Progressive disclosure exposes metadata for a growing skill library, introducing context pollution; retrieval limits catalog exposure, but static retrieval from the initial task query may be misaligned with capability needs that emerge during reasoning and may still inject unnecessary skills. We propose SkillBrowser, a Meta-Skill-managed framework that enables an agent to identify current capability needs, formulate targeted queries, and selectively load skills on demand. Benefits remain model- and task-dependent, with under-invocation of useful skills and additional latency as key limitations. These results demonstrate the viability of Meta-Skill-managed SSE for scalable skill repositories.

## 7. Trapped in Your Story: Evaluating Narrative-Induced Sycophancy in Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Runtai Yang , Dandan Zhu , Kaiwei Zhang , Guangtao Zhai
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23525](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23525)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) are known to exhibit sycophancy by adjusting their responses toward users' expressed views. However, real users often justify their opinions through reasons, personal experiences, causal explanations, and broader narratives, raising the question of whether such narratives can further steer model judgments. We construct a controlled benchmark of 500 debatable questions across ten domains, where the user stance is held fixed while its justification is systematically varied without introducing new task-relevant evidence. Each question is instantiated under 11 matched conditions: one neutral baseline and five mirrored narrative levels for each of two opposing positions, yielding 5,500 evaluation instances. We evaluate seven LLMs and observe substantial differences in narrative-induced agreement. The effect is not monotonic with narrative elaboration: personal experience can reduce agreement, whereas causal framing and worldview-based synthesis increase it. Controlled GPT-5.1 ablations further show that causal framing produces the strongest positive effect, while claim-preserving bullet presentation also increases agreement over paragraph form. These findings suggest that sycophancy is shaped not only by user stance, but also by how supporting explanations are structured and presented.

## 8. LocalIntent: Outcome-Specific Structured Generation for Backend-Compatible Smart Building Automation with Local LLMs

## 9. S-GAP: Stealthy Graph Attack Primitives for Graph Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Wenda Li , Tongya Zheng , Shunyu Liu , Hanyang Yuan , Kaixuan Chen , Yu Wang , Yuchen Ying , Haoze Jiang , Huan Li , Gang Chen , Zujie Ren
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23465](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23465)
- **Abstract**:
> Graph Large Language Models (GraphLLMs) enable powerful semantic understanding and reasoning over Text-Attributed Graphs (TAGs), while their semantic reasoning may exhibit vulnerabilities to perturbations in node text and graph structure under adversarial attack evaluation. Existing attacks typically construct textual and structural perturbations in separate stages guided by distinct realization intents. However, this separation results in attack-intent fragmentation, causing semantic incoherence between the two perturbation types, thereby undermining attack stealth and making them easier for GraphLLMs to identify and disregard, ultimately weakening attack effectiveness. To avoid attack-intent fragmentation, we propose the Graph Attack Primitive (GAP), an atomic semantic representation that unifies textual and structural perturbations under a coherent attack intent. By carefully calibrating the semantic composition of a GAP, we can achieve strong attack effectiveness while maintaining sufficient attack stealth. Furthermore, we introduce S-GAP (Stealthy GAP), a multi-agent framework that iteratively refines the semantic content of the GAP. The framework unifies GAP planning and realization and continuously refines this process through surrogate evaluation and textual feedback. Extensive experiments across four datasets, three GraphLLMs, and two attack settings show that S-GAP improves ASR over the strongest baselines by 7.02% on average while producing more relationally plausible perturbations, exposing critical vulnerabilities in current GraphLLMs and motivating more robust defenses.

## 11. Generative Policy Improvement from Observational Data
- **Authors**: Jonas Schweisthal , Dennis Frauen , Stefan Feuerriegel , Emma Brunskill
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23436](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23436)
- **Abstract**:
> We study how to improve decision policies from observational data to generate better actions when actions are complex, structured objects, such as text messages or images. This task is subject to two major challenges: (i) action-level overlap is often too weak for reliable off-policy evaluation and improvement, and (ii) generating new structured actions often requires expensive training or fine-tuning (e.g., of an LLM) and can be ill-defined due to information loss in action representations. To address both, we propose PolicyLift, a three-stage framework for generative policy improvement with structured actions from observational data. In Stage 1, PolicyLift learns a small set of discrete action codes that capture high-level strategies in the observed action space and are tailored to support policy improvement under limited overlap. In Stage 2, it learns a context-dependent policy over these action codes. At deployment time, Stage 3 "lifts" the learned code-level policy back to the original action space by generating actions from the historical behavior system in a code-conditioned way. We show theoretically that improving the policy over action codes guarantees improvement of the induced policy in the original action space. We demonstrate PolicyLift across experiments in synthetic and real-world text generation tasks with consistent policy improvements over the behavior policy.

## 12. Monitor-Validity Failures: LLM Safety Evidence Paths at Evolving Framework Boundaries
- **Authors**: Junchi Liao
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23362](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23362)
- **Abstract**:
> Modern LLM applications rely on runtime monitors that inspect tool calls, traces, streams, detector outputs, and verifier inputs. This creates an interface-reliability problem: a framework path may remain operational while policy-relevant evidence never reaches its decision point. In one representative case, a regex detector returned a positive match, but the framework stranded the result before the block decision. This differs from jailbreaks and detector errors because the evidence is already recognized yet disconnected from enforcement. We study this repair-boundary signature with canary contracts: parent/fixed confirmation units that place a known payload only on the changed evidence surface and record monitor visibility together with the downstream decision for action-boundary cases. Nine are established-boundary repairs, eight are new-surface policy-coverage gaps, and one is a boundary-only evidence case. Fourteen reach E1 enforcement boundaries: seven native action outcomes, six configured enforcement boundaries, and one synthetic-consumer consequence. The remaining cases affect verifier, safety-prompt, or observability channels. The cases form carrier, adapter, authority, and surface families and show how ordinary application behavior can remain intact while a safety-specific evidence path misses its decision. Exact revisions, deterministic outcomes, negative controls, and a frozen prospective audit document the confirmation rule. Monitor assumptions therefore need regression tests at the schemas, streams, tools, and adapters that frameworks evolve.

## 13. Robust Function Calling for LLM Agents Under Interface Drift

- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23342](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23342)
- **Abstract**:

## 15. Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT): Evidence-Based Text Generation via a Closed-Loop Retrieve--Reflect--Revise Pipeline on Edge LLMs

- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23311](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23311)
- **Abstract**:

We introduce Self-Reflective Chain-of-Thought (SRCoT), a modular architecture that organizes generation as a closed Retrieve--Reflect--Revise loop. SRCoT separates environment state, persistent knowledge, accumulated experience, and short-term memory into provenance-labeled stores. BM25 retrieval supplies local context; subsequent passes expose intermediate drafts, missing-information markers, follow-up queries, and optional claim--evidence mappings. ## 16. CoTA++: Understanding the Repeat Curse in dMLLMs from an Information Flow Perspective
- **Authors**: Qiyan Zhao , Xiaofeng Zhang , Xu-Yao Zhang , Shijie Han , Zhepeng Li , Shunzhi Zhu , Da-Han Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23290](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23290)
- **Abstract**:
> Diffusion-based multimodal large language models (dMLLMs) decode by iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, which precludes the standard autoregressive key--value cache. Practical deployments therefore rely on approximate caching that reuses intermediate states across denoising steps. Such caching, however, often makes a response repeat one token at adjacent positions in a run that breaks the sentence, a failure we term the \emph{Repeat Curse}. Tracing the context-token information flow reveals three disruptions. Within a step, caching disperses the attention that anchors a position on nearby context tokens. Across steps, similarity-based refresh is not staleness-aware, so some positions are decoded against stale key--value states. Across layers, the deep-layer entropy of the context fails to converge where repetition arises. We accordingly propose CoTA++, a training-free, plug-and-play framework that counteracts each disruption where it originates: Context-Token Attention Re-anchoring re-forms the attention routing against the current context, Decode-Aware Refresh reserves part of the cache's refresh budget for the positions about to be committed, and Context-Token Entropy-Guided Voting discounts the confidence of a position by the deep-layer entropy of its context, which reorders the unmasking. The three components act at non-overlapping stages of a decoding step and compose freely with existing dMLLMs and caches. Across three dMLLMs and two caching backends, CoTA++ reduces the sample repetition rate of long responses from up to 99.6\% to at most 17.0\%, recovers most of the accuracy lost to caching on nine benchmarks, and keeps the speedup of caching at under 15\% overhead, making approximate caching reliable for dMLLMs.

## 17. Annotrace: A Workbench for Step-Level Annotation of Agent Traces
- **Authors**: Arun Josephraj Arokiaraj , Chenyi Huang , Sahan Bulathwela , Zekun Wu , Bin Wu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23284](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.23284)
- **Abstract**:
> Every study of why LLM agents fail begins by labelling traces by hand, and every such study builds its own throwaway tool. Observability platforms record agent traces but offer no structured labelling; annotation tools label text but have no notion of a step, a delegation or a tool call, leaving it unclear what the smallest unit of execution to label should be. Annotrace reads six trace formats, including OpenTelemetry GenAI, LangSmith and Langfuse, and turns each run into an ordered, nested list of steps. ## 22. Where Does the Performance Go? Understanding and Recovering Performance Regressions in C-to-Rust Translation

> Large language model (LLM) agents for health can accumulate reusable textual skills from task feedback, such as calculation criteria, measurement time points, and formula units. Deciding which skills to retain is consequential: useful rules improve later answers, while faulty generalizations propagate errors. We propose CausalEvo, a method for causal skill self-evolution that estimates the relative effects of deliberately changed skill sets while holding the model weights, feedback cases, and rule count fixed. At each evolution step, a frozen LLM proposes candidate rules, which are pooled with the incumbent skills so that the current skill set remains a feasible choice. Paired skill replacements against shared background rules are scored on the same feedback cases; least squares converts the score differences into relative skill values, and the top-ranked rules are retained, favoring incumbents on ties. A decision-loss bound separates candidate support, approximation, and estimation errors. On six MedCalc-Bench-Verified tasks with 120 held-out cases, CausalEvo achieves 60.00% accuracy, compared with 55.83% for SkillOpt and 51.67% without skills. Across the recorded training configurations, which use different optimization budgets, it consumes 55.6% fewer training tokens than SkillOpt. Gains concentrate in particular calculations alongside regressions on others, and paired accuracy intervals include zero.
