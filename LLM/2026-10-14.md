## 1. MFG: Multi-turn Feedback Guided Molecular Optimization
- **Authors**: Trieu Nguyen , Frazier N. Baker , Ruoxi Gao , Daniel Adu-Ampratwum , Xia Ning
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18851](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18851)
- **Abstract**:
> Multi-objective molecular optimization is a critical process in drug discovery that aims to modify a given molecule to simultaneously improve multiple properties while maintaining a similar overall structure. This process often requires iterative modifications guided by computational or experimental feedback. Recent large language model (LLM)-based methods have begun to explore this iterative approach; however, these methods are not explicitly trained to leverage such feedback, while existing approaches primarily focus on single-turn molecular optimization. To address this gap, we introduce Multi-turn Feedback Guided (MFG) molecular optimization, a reinforcement learning framework that trains an LLM to progressively optimize molecules using feedback from previous turns. MFG introduces a feedback vector capturing both property improvements and source molecule similarity, alongside a feedback-aligned reward function that promotes progressive improvement across turns. We apply MFG over $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O}$, a state-of-the-art LLM model for multi-objective molecular optimization, evaluate $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O-MFG}$ on ten multi-objective molecular optimization tasks. The results demonstrate that $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O-MFG}$ achieves the highest average similarity-constrained success rate ($81.50%$), outperforming the base model $\text{GeLLM}^{3}\text{O}$ ($70.81%$).

## 2. Read the Room Before Your Model Generates
- **Authors**: Muyao Kong , Zhihao Lin
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18820](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18820)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) generally rely on sampling temperature to regulate the amount of randomness in their outputs. Mainstream methods for selecting temperature typically involve fixed, heuristic global settings and context or token-level calibration, rather than a geometrically interpretable representation of the communicative scenario. In this work, we introduce a simple and interpretable temperature selection framework, the \textbf{Scenario-Actor}, which adapts decoding to the inferred situational characteristics. Scenario-Actor extracts scenario-relevant structures from Transformer hidden states and determines the temperature from scenario embedding, a structured geometric representation of the communicative setting. Empirical results show that on Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct, Scenario-Actor achieves strong goal-completion performance in interactive social scenarios, significant gains on open-ended instruction following, and competitive performance on multi-turn conversation compared to multiple baselines.

## 3. BLADE: Auditing Information Use for Failure Attribution in LLM Multi-Agent Systems
- **Authors**: Xiaoguang Guo , Yaohao Long , Chuxu Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18780)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model multi-agent systems extend individual agents' capabilities through collaboration across specialized roles, tools, and workflows. Yet collaboration makes failures harder to diagnose: one agent may misinterpret a correct result while others carry the mistake forward. Failure attribution therefore seeks to identify the responsible agent and decisive error step. Recent methods trace dependencies, but knowing where information came from is not the same as knowing where it went wrong. We introduce BLADE (Blame Localization via Audited Dependency Evidence), a training-free framework that moves beyond tracing information to auditing its use. Using Jev for structured probabilistic judgments, BLADE uses dependency probabilities to select relevant source-decision handoffs for auditing. At these handoffs, it examines whether receiving decisions use their sources faithfully and within task requirements, distinguishing error introduction, propagation, and repair. These findings inform a global diagnosis that considers all execution steps. Without reference answers, BLADE achieves the best or tied-best step accuracy among eight strategies under a shared Jev decision interface on 1,228 failed runs across five Who&When and Who&When Pro subsets, outperforming All-at-once by 2.2-11.1 percentage points. Making responsibility evidence explicit offers a path toward more interpretable and reliable agentic systems.

## 4. Beyond the Verdict: Agentic Code Judging with Verifiable Feedback
- **Authors**: Zheye Deng , Sha Li , Changlong Yu , Xin Liu , Qin Lu , Priyanka Nigam , Yangqiu Song
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18760](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18760)
- **Abstract**:
> Test-time scaling for large language models (LLMs) can involve generating multiple candidate solutions for selection or revising an earlier attempt. To guide selection and revision, we need a verifier that can estimate candidate correctness and provide evidence of failure. In competitive programming, however, official judging systems provide authoritative verdicts under finite submission budgets, without revealing failure explanations or failing inputs. To provide informative feedback without consuming official submissions, we formulate a task in which a model judges and diagnoses a candidate program using only the problem statement and source code, with generated counterexamples serving as checkable evidence. To evaluate this task, we build USACO-JUDGE from the USACO 2026 contests, using accepted and unaccepted programs written by human experts and multiple LLMs. We then train ARBITER, a 35B mixture-of-experts (MoE) agentic verifier, with reinforcement learning in a generic execution sandbox. Training raises counterexample success on incorrect programs from 40.61% to 62.67% and mean binary verdict accuracy on USACO-JUDGE from 71.41% to 87.41%, approaching frontier verification performance. Beyond verification, ARBITER outperforms baselines in both code selection and refinement. In an IOI 2026 case study, ARBITER-guided refinement raises GPT-OSS-120B's expected score above the gold-medal threshold under the official submission limit.

## 5. Unlocking Token Rewards via Training-Free Reward Attribution
- **Authors**: Sitong Wu , Haoru Tan , Bin Xia , Xichen Zhang , Jingyao Li , Shaofeng Zhang , Xiaojuan Qi , Bei Yu , Jiaya Jia
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18704](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18704)
- **Abstract**:
> In this paper, we propose an extremely efficient, training-free method to extract token-level reward signals directly from an existing deep reward model. Our core idea is to attribute the overall process reward to individual tokens by estimating each token's influence. This influence is defined as the change in the final macroscopic reward (e.g., the process reward) when a token is replaced with a semantically null token. Naively calculating this influence is computationally infeasible, requiring N forward passes through the PRM for an N-token sequence. We overcome this bottleneck by proposing a highly efficient gradient-based estimator. Specifically, we use a first-order Taylor approximation, which simplifies the influence calculation to the inner product of the difference between the token embedding and the null token embedding, and the gradient of the reward with respect to the token embedding. This requires only a single forward and backward pass. The resulting token-level rewards enable standard RL algorithms to perform precise credit assignment without requiring additional reward model training. Experiments on challenging reasoning benchmarks demonstrate that our method substantially improves policy optimization efficiency and enhances the generalization of LLM reasoning capabilities. Our P2T outperforms the outcome reward by +4.9% on MathVista for Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct, and +11.5% on AIME24 for Qwen2.5-Math-7B. The code will be released via this https URL .

## 6. To Align or Not To Align: Check Your COMPASS Before You Train
- **Authors**: Fabian Gröger , Petar Damjanović , Maria Brbić
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18638](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18638)
- **Abstract**:
> Combining pretrained models is a common strategy for building AI systems, whether by aligning a vision encoder with a language model or merging fine-tuned checkpoints. However, finding compatible pairs typically requires costly empirical search, as compatibility is known only after alignment or merging. We propose COMPASS (COMPatibility ASsessment Score), a theoretically motivated diagnostic that estimates whether a model pair will be compatible prior to alignment or merging. Building on the observation that representational convergence is most robust at the level of local neighborhoods, COMPASS decomposes model compatibility into two cheap-to-compute axes: a pair-specific ease, defined as the $k$-NN overlap between the two encoders, and a paradigm-level ceiling, capturing the maximum overlap their training paradigms allow. Together, these two axes provide a ranking of candidate model pairs and prescribe how to act on each pair: (i) prioritize pairs that already agree and whose paradigms are compatible, (ii) invest additional compute on pairs with compatible paradigms but low initial agreement, (iii) switch the pretraining paradigm when it cannot support sufficient agreement, or (iv) deprioritize pairs that score poorly on both axes. We apply COMPASS to $72$ cross-modal alignment experiments and $392$ task-arithmetic model merges and show that compatibility estimates by COMPASS strongly correlate with downstream success, achieving average Pearson correlation of $0.91$ on cross-modal alignment tasks and $0.70$ on model merging.

## 7. Can an Agent Learn to Predict Its Own Failure? Outcome-Grounded Confidence Calibration in LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Ashutosh Hathidara
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18614)
- **Abstract**:
> Controlling an acting LLM agent means deciding, at each step of a long-horizon task, whether to trust its next action, abstain, or hand off to a human. That decision needs a per-step confidence signal predicting whether the current rollout will eventually succeed. The agent's own self-reported verbalized confidence is the natural candidate, but existing reinforcement learning methods calibrate it against the correctness of a decision at the current step. We propose a method to calibrate it against the eventual outcome of a multi-step episode: under GRPO, the group of rollouts for a task supplies success labels, and a proper scoring rule scores each step's confidence against the realized outcome. In offline RL on recorded trajectories of a Qwen2.5-7B agent in ALFWorld, TextWorld, and WebShop environments, the AUROC of step confidence against episode outcome rises from near chance to 0.68-0.85, and the top-10% of steps the agent is most confident about succeed ~4.3x more often than average. In online RL on the live ALFWorld agent, four calibration rewards improve early-step AUROC, expected calibration error goes from 0.42 to below 0.12, and selective execution improves over both the untrained agent and a format-only RL control, without degrading task success. We also introduce an evaluation protocol that separates episode depth, task difficulty, and attempt-level foresight, which shows where the gain lies: the trained agent recognizes from its first step which tasks it will fail, while telling a succeeding attempt from a failing one at the same task is harder, with some early-window signal emerging on TextWorld.

## 8. Beyond Answer Voting: Fine-Grained Correction of LLM Reasoning through Graph Alignment across Sampled Traces
- **Authors**: Haotian Liu , Yingjian Chen , Xiaohang Xu , Lang Huang , Yusuke Iwasawa , Yutaka Matsuo , Irene Li
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18606](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18606)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models increasingly tackle tasks that require multi-step reasoning, where errors in intermediate claims can affect the final answer. Self-consistency (SC) improves reasoning by voting over sampled final answers, but its vote leaves intermediate disagreements unresolved. To address this problem, we introduce DDRG (Disagreement-Driven Verification Guided by Reasoning Graphs), a framework for selective correction from a frozen bank of reasoning traces. Our insight is that graph alignment can expose claim-level disagreements and provide correction evidence without expanding the sampled trace bank. DDRG builds source-grounded claim graphs and sends localized conflicts to an answer-blind verifier. A verifier-supported refutation that reaches the majority answer's supporting path triggers one focused repair; otherwise, DDRG returns the exact SC vote. Experimental results across five benchmarks show that native zero-shot DDRG improves macro-average accuracy over direct answering by 8.65 and 15.83 percentage points for Qwen2.5-7B and Llama3.1-8B. It also exceeds CoT, the evaluated GoT and GoV question-answering adaptations, and SC on this macro metric. DDRG links local verification to selective correction without enlarging the initial candidate bank.

## 9. Compare to Correct: Pairwise-Guided Logit Adjustment for LLM Test-Time Scaling
- **Authors**: Cong Hua , Qianqian Xu , Zitai Wang , Zhiyong Yang , Shilong Bao , Linchao Zhang , Qingming Huang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18583](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18583)
- **Abstract**:
> Sampling-based test-time scaling enhances LLM reasoning by aggregating multiple reasoning paths for the same query. Existing methods use answer frequencies or path confidence to compute candidate scores, which do not explicitly characterize comparisons between competing answers. Inspired by the Condorcet principle in social choice theory, we introduce pairwise confidence comparisons between paths supporting different answers to capture answer-level comparative advantage. We combine this comparative evidence with voting support through a KL-regularized objective. Theoretically, we show that this comparative-advantage is equivalent to the adjustment to the traditional voting logits. In this way, our proposed Pairwise-Guided Logit Adjustment (PGLA) framework allows comparative evidence to compensate for weaker voting support and correct voting-based ranking errors. Experiments on 10 challenging benchmarks including mathematical reasoning, knowledge reasoning, and code generation with 7 models ranging from 1.7B to 32B parameters speak to the effectiveness of this plug-and-play term. Additional evaluations with external reward scores and online sampling further demonstrate its applicability.

## 10. LLM Agents for Automated Post-Training via Structured Recipe Optimization
- **Authors**: Ruohong Liu , Bohan Tang , Lekang Jiang , Jack Umenberger , Yiwen Guo
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18572)
- **Abstract**:
> Automated post-training transforms pre-trained large language models (LLMs) into models capable of handling diverse domains by automatically constructing and optimizing a recipe of coupled decisions over data sources, data processing, cross-domain composition, and training configuration under a limited experimental budget. Existing automation either searches predefined recipe spaces that enable systematic optimization but remain fixed rather than adapting their available choices to the target tasks, or uses open-ended agents that discover task-specific choices but leave them implicit in trial-level edits, limiting their systematic reuse and recombination across trials. To fill this gap, we introduce optimization over a task-adapted recipe space, a novel paradigm that can adapt the recipe space itself to the target tasks while representing its choices explicitly for systematic reuse, recombination, and joint optimization. We instantiate this paradigm with Structured Recipe Optimization (SRO), comprising an LLM-based Recipe-Space Designer that constructs the task-adapted recipe space and a Multi-Domain Recipe Optimizer that uses structured trial evidence to guide LLM candidate construction and multi-objective Bayesian trial selection. Extensive experiments show that SRO achieves the highest average score in all evaluated target-model and agent-LLM combinations. In joint post-training across three capability domains and six benchmarks, SRO reaches 41.1 versus 15.4 for the strongest baseline on Qwen3-1.7B with GPT-5.4, and 53.8 versus 40.8 on Qwen3-4B with DeepSeek-V4-Flash. Our code is available at this https URL .

## 11. Configuring RAG Systems: What Breaks, Where Originates, and How to Fix
- **Authors**: Liangyu Chen , Youpeng Ma , Yulong Ye , Zezhen Xiang , Tao Chen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18567](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18567)
- **Abstract**:
> Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) has emerged as a dominant paradigm for improving Large Language Model (LLM) by intertwining it with other components, such as a vector/graph database. Therefore, the resulting RAG systems are often highly configurable, under which a non-trivial amount of failures are related to the configurations. However, there remains a lack of systematic understanding regarding the configuration issues for RAG systems. To bridge this gap, in this paper, we conduct the first large-scale empirical study of 654 real-world configuration issues collected from 10 diverse RAG systems. We also provide nine actionable insights to the practitioners on testing, localizing, and fixing the configuration issues for RAG systems.

## 12. Rethinking Quality in Large Language Model Routing: Security-Conditioned Model-and-Budget Selection
- **Authors**: Yichi Zhang , Zhenxiang Huang , Yuyang Zhang , Yuchen Yang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18566)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) routers reduce serving cost by choosing which model to call and how many output tokens to grant it for each query. Existing routers rank model-budget actions using a single quality metric. This does not work for mixed workloads. For ordinary queries, a good answer is correct and useful (utility); for security-sensitive queries, a good answer refuses harmful requests or resists malicious instructions (security). These two objectives prefer different models and budgets. We propose SCRouter, a router that learns when to prioritize security and how to allocate the token budget. For each model-budget pair, it estimates the probability that the response finishes within budget. It uses this probability to predict utility and security separately. A small probe determines how to weight these two predictions for each query. SCRouter then selects the model and budget by balancing the weighted utility and security against cost. We also build SCRouterBench, a benchmark of 38,869 utility and security queries spanning nine security dimensions, each answered under all 144 combinations of 12 models and 12 budgets, plus 2,753 out-of-domain queries. SCRouter improves the baselines by 13-29 points in-domain and 19-23 points out-of-domain.

## 13. Lost in Implicit Routing: How LLM Agents Fail to Load Relevant Skills at Scale
- **Authors**: Jinlin Liu , Wei Chen , Xiaojin Zhang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18522](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18522)
- **Abstract**:
> Reusable skills let LLM agents load task-specific instructions on demand, but they make execution depend on implicit routing over skill metadata. We test whether this design scales by holding SkillsBench tasks and their golden skills fixed while adding irrelevant skills. We find skill routing collapse: agents increasingly load fewer or no skills, even though the relevant skills remain available. With 1,000 irrelevant skills, loaded-skill recall falls from 73.6% to 27.4% and the zero-load rate rises from 9.3% to 51.6%, whereas explicit selection from the same library retains 73.9% recall. Loading other skills, including plausible alternatives to golden skills, is too rare to explain this decline. Masking only the descriptions of irrelevant skills largely restores skill loading, yet with the metadata intact, explicitly asking agents to plan skill use still yields lower recall than preselection. These findings point to semantic interference from descriptions and instruction interference when skill choice competes with task execution. We also evaluate explicit preselection as a practical mitigation, including its coverage and latency trade-offs. Our analysis informs skill protocols and evaluation for agents with growing skill libraries.

## 14. Judges Follow the Reference: The Derivation Gap in Verifying Tool-Call Arguments
- **Authors**: Linhai Ma , Yan Wang , Lingfei Qian , Yupeng Cao , Victor Gutierrez Basulto
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18497](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18497)
- **Abstract**:
> Tool-call judges evaluate argument values proposed by LLM agents. We find that they accept a correct value less often when their input holds no reference for it, that is, no text that says what the value should be. This happens when the value is a default value from the function schema that the user did not mention, when it has to be inferred, or when the text it comes from was cut from the judge input. We call this drop in acceptance the derivation gap. It appears across all seven judges we evaluate, including System One models CLM and Jev. The gap is not only a truncation artifact: correct default values have the largest gap on a benchmark with no truncation. A controlled source-relocation test shows that judges follow the reference in their input: they favor a visible incorrect value over a correct value whose source is hidden. We introduce DeRef (Derived Reference): a separate model reads the full history without seeing the candidate and writes a reference for the judge. Across three benchmarks, DeRef improves overall argument AUROC for all judges tested. On the controlled test, it raises AUROC from chance or below to between 98.40 and 99.59.

## 16. Choosing LLMs for Strategic Agent Interactions
- **Authors**: Arastun Mammadli , Herbert Woisetschläger , Jawad Fayaz , Shiqiang Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18354](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18354)
- **Abstract**:
> Game-theoretic benchmarks claim that large language model (LLM) strategic decision-making varies across game types, interaction structures, and capability dimensions. However, they only evaluate strategic performance across individual fixed models. In parallel, contemporary LLM routers pick models under quality-cost trade-offs, but with no notion of a strategic structure. They do not account for an opponent that might be unknown, and that can adapt over time and shift the feedback signal. To address this, we extend the game-theoretic evaluations and define strategic selection as the meta game layered on top of the base game. In each round, a strategic selector fields an LLM from a portfolio of models to navigate a game payoff matrix that is latent and learned online. We find that the evaluation signal is game-specific, and that extracting a reliable signal requires a much higher number of seed runs $N$ compared to traditional benchmarking setups. Evaluating across 5 games and 10 LLMs, we show a model's strategic competence varies significantly depending on the game setup and the opponent faced. Building on this, we implement a suite of selector methods and find that approaches that rely on query embeddings or LLM self-reflection do not consistently capture the underlying strategic signal. A lightweight multi-armed bandit algorithm performs more reliably across games, though it still falls short of the best fixed model. Finally, we show that simple non-stationary and adversarial bandit variants fail to outperform a stationary bandit baseline, pointing to a gap in how current methods handle a shifting opponent in strategic settings.

## 18. Output Discipline Sets the Floor: A Falsification-Guided, Model-Agnostic Agent Protocol for Auditable Hypothesis-Driven Decision Making
- **Authors**: Jing Liu , Yongxing Qi , Ping Yao , Ting Wu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18298)
- **Abstract**:
> What binds large language models on auditable, hypothesis-driven decision tasks, where every candidate can be checked against the evidence? On the public TCMEval-SDT benchmark for Traditional Chinese Medicine syndrome differentiation (200 cases), the binding constraint is output discipline and reasoning structure, not model capability alone: two years of model scaling move zero-shot scores by only -0.03 to +0.08, while output-discipline failures (non-terminating reasoning, format non-compliance, large run-to-run spread) persist even in frontier models. We test this with a model-agnostic agent framework formalizing decision making as a five-stage closed loop, enforced by a candidate x evidence matrix falsification protocol: every candidate is judged against every evidence item, at least half of the candidates must be falsified before a conclusion is licensed, and survivors are emitted in a machine-verifiable single-line format. Across five base models, the effect is sign-conditional on zero-shot output discipline: it rescues models whose discipline fails (DeepSeek-V4-Flash: +0.267/+0.271, entirely termination rescue), reduces a high-variance model's run-to-run standard deviation from 0.218 to 0.007 (Kimi K3), and significantly costs already-disciplined models (-0.087 to -0.120 on budget-matched Qwen3-4B/32B). Ablations attribute the cost to the candidate-bounded format layer and show the effect is independent of injected knowledge content (protocol-knowledge decoupling). The protocol also transfers to misinformation-robust mathematical reasoning, detecting injected false premises at 89.8% recall with a 6.8% false-positive rate (MCC 0.54; a flag-everything baseline scores 0) and restoring accuracy to the no-misinformation ceiling. Where zero-shot output discipline is weak, the reasoning protocol sets the floor; base-model capability always sets the ceiling.

## 19. Beyond Answer Sharing: TrueMAD for Targeted Peer Engagement in Multi-Agent Debate
- **Authors**: Dezheng Bao , Sihang Du , Renhong Huang , Zhizhang Yuan , Yifei Sun , Yang Yang

- **Abstract**:

Analyses at the utterance, agent-transition, and group levels further show more peer-specific deliberation, more correction-oriented revisions, and more favorable consensus dynamics.

## 20. RadixFlow: Filling Execution Bubbles in Prefix-Cached Hybrid LLM Serving
- **Authors**: Huihao Jing , Wenbin Hu , Shaojin Chen , Haochen Shi , Haoyu Huang , Sirui Zhang , Yuxuan Liu , Zhongwei Xie , Haoran Li
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18143](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18143)
- **Abstract**:
> Prefix reuse improves LLM inference by sharing the KV cache of common prompt prefixes. This helps only if the scheduler can execute a matched branch in the current worker round. Schedulers assume that a matched prefix is ready, which holds for full attention but fails for hybrid LLMs. There, a branch (the consumer) may need recurrent state that another branch (the producer) is still computing in the same round. The native scheduler stops its waiting-queue scan at this blocked consumer, and the resulting head-of-line blocking creates \emph{execution bubbles}: idle batch slots that independent work could fill. We introduce RadixFlow, which decouples prefix matching from state readiness. Its state-ready traversal turns a global scan barrier into a local wait: only the consumer is blocked until the next round, while feasible independent branches keep filling the batch under the native state check. On five hybrid models, RadixFlow lowers mean latency by 3\% to 24\% and worker rounds by 11\% to 44\% at almost unchanged reuse. On the larger Qwen3.5 models, ablations show that most of the gain comes from traversal, which adds little on a full-attention control. Heavier rounds limit how much of this saving reaches latency.

## 21. Mitigating Cross-Modal Hallucinations by Correcting Modality Misrouting in Omnimodal LLMs
- **Authors**: Yu Mi , Licheng Pan , Jiayi Zhu , Qirui Liu , Yitong Qiao , Changtong Xu , Tao Wei , Fei Sun , Zhixuan Chu , Kui Ren
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18141)
- **Abstract**:
> Omnimodal large language models (OLLMs) are susceptible to cross-modal hallucinations under audio-visual interference. We find that, for a substantial fraction of incorrect joint predictions, the question-relevant unimodal branch can still recover the correct answer, suggesting that task-relevant evidence can remain recoverable from the relevant modality even when it is not reflected in the joint prediction. We refer to this recoverable failure pattern as \emph{modality misrouting}. ## 223. Checking the Checkers: A Synthesis of Verifier Noise and Robustness in RLVR
- **Authors**: Savyasachi Gupta , Aniruddha Srinivas Joshi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17824](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17824)
- **Abstract**:

It rests on the assumption that the checker scoring each response is itself correct. In practice, rule-based checkers reject correct answers in unexpected formats, unit tests accept incorrect programs, and LLM judges can be led to approve empty or adversarial responses.
