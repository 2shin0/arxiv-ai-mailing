## 1. Can Language Models Play the Wikipedia Game?
- **Authors**: Alexander Hickey , Joanna Knight , James Bishop
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13948](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13948)
- **Abstract**:
> Language models increasingly support or automate information retrieval from document collections too large to index. This task is difficult when it requires specialist linguistic or scientific knowledge. We used the Wikipedia Game to test language models on an analogous information retrieval task. In the game, players must reach a target article using only article links. We compared sentence embedding models and LLMs across five Wikipedia languages and five academic-literature datasets. We construct very large local graphs of Wikipedia pages and of paper citations using OpenAlex and provide code to build and navigate them. This allows the study of language models' navigation capabilities without relying on third-party APIs. We compared the models with human and TF-IDF benchmarks. The Wikipedia Game format enables robust comparisons across techniques, languages, and domains. No model significantly outperformed humans, although the best models (GPT-o3 mini, GPT-4o, QwQ:32B, and DeepSeek-R1:70B) achieved comparable success. Performance and rankings varied across languages and topics, with implications for practitioners utilising these techniques.

## 2. Studying Comics at Scale with Artificial Intelligence: The PAGE Framework and Three Studies of American Comics and Japanese Manga
- **Authors**: Emilio Ferrara
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13840](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13840)
- **Abstract**:
> Comics are one of the great visual records of the twentieth century: hundreds of thousands of pages in which a mass readership saw its leaders, its wars and its heroes. Most of this record has never been studied as a whole; comics scholarship proceeds one book at a time. We argue that artificial intelligence, including generative models, can change this to the benefit of the medium itself. We propose PAGE, a framework that makes artificial intelligence an instrument for comics research. It parses every scan into panels, text and a bibliographic record; annotates each page with a vision-language model that follows a written codebook, as a human coder would; grounds samples of these readings in human annotation; and explains change across an archive, with estimates controlled for publisher and genre. We demonstrate the framework on 3,929 American comic books (1938--1956) and 87 volumes of Japanese manga, in three studies, each resting on a different capability. The first pairs text search with visual reading to recover how a mass medium portrayed political power. The second measures the page's visual form across an industry and recovers the hand of individual artists. The third follows who speaks across two comics traditions, checked against human annotation. Beyond research, the same instruments can make archives searchable by what is drawn, open comics to blind and low-vision readers, and help preserve fragile collections and credit uncredited artists. We outline a research agenda in which artificial intelligence strengthens the study, preservation and reach of the medium.

## 5. A Multi-Agent LLM System for Automated Tuning of Specialized Neural Network Models
- **Authors**: Ivan Moloshnikov , Dmitry Kunitsyn , Alexander Sboev , Maksim Skorokhodov , Roman Rybka , Yuanchao Liu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13663)
- **Abstract**:
> Many machine learning approaches are two-level: an expert selects a class of methods, within this class the choice of key components is critical and usually manual. This paper addresses the automatic tuning of these components and proposes a framework transferable across models of different nature. The multi-agent large language model (LLM) framework has three roles---analyst, coder, and recorder~---with an experiment log and a sandboxed execution environment. Unlike systems that optimize the entire ML script or search architectures in a discrete space, the framework operates over an open but semantically narrow space: the agent generates code only for the specified components, leaving the overall network structure unchanged. The approach is validated in two directions: tuning a classification spiking neural network with correlation learning, and solving high-order differential equations with Physics-Informed Neural Networks (PINN). Across five tasks---spiking classification on MNIST and Digits, and boundary-value problems for the generalized nonlinear Schrödinger equation (GNLSE) and Kuramoto--Sivashinsky (KS) equations---the agent outperforms the baselines: classification accuracy is 13 percentage points (MNIST) and 6 percentage points (Digits) higher; the error against the analytical single-soliton solution is reduced approximately 2491-fold; the invariant-preservation error drops approximately 325-fold in the selected GNLSE run with a Gaussian perturbation and by more than 100-fold on average over repeated initializations; and the mass drift drops 14-fold for the KS problem with a Gaussian perturbation. The framework thus tunes key components on tasks of different nature without changing its internal structure.

## 6. Humanize Olympiad Agents: A Multi-Session Workflow for Olympiad Problem Solving with Solve, Review, Wiki, Verify
- **Authors**: Zhengyang Zhang , Jing Xiong , Hongzhou Lin , Juihui Chung , Zijian Zhang , Song Han , Sihao Liu , Ligeng Zhu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13632](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13632)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) agents are rapidly improving on long-horizon scientific and olympiad tasks. Recent systems have produced a Lean-formalized resolution of the Navier--Stokes problem and autonomous solutions to four open questions in the Erdős Conjectures database. Yet neither system releases both its underlying model and complete inference stack. More broadly, a single agent session remains brittle on difficult problems: early errors can propagate, missing domain knowledge can stall progress, and apparent success can mask a misinterpretation or weakening of the original task. We introduce HOA (Humanize Olympiads Agent), an open, flow-level framework that coordinates multiple agent sessions through three complementary feedback channels. First, an independent reviewer evaluates each candidate and provides actionable feedback. Second, a structured wiki supplies task-specific knowledge that may be missing or unreliable in model pretraining. Third, a verifier checks generated Lean proofs against trusted challenge statements. With GPT-5.6 Sol, HOA achieves perfect scores on the IMO, IPhO, and IOI 2026, near-full marks on the IChO 2026 problem set, and solves all 672 problems (100%) on PutnamBench. On the Lean-Eval leaderboard, HOA ranks first, solving 251 of 304 problems (82.6%). We further evaluate HOA with open models. Using Kimi-K3, HOA achieves full scores on the IMO, IPhO, and IOI, and a gold-level score of 97.1% on the IChO. These results closely match the closed-source GPT-5.6 Sol + /Goal baseline on the IMO and IPhO, while exceeding it on the IChO and IOI, demonstrating that systematic feedback can help bridge substantial differences in base-model capability.

## 7. Retrieval-Augmented Multimodal Language Model with Heterogeneous Evidence Integration for Clinical Prognosis
- **Authors**: Hamza Shafiq , Jialu Tang , Ting Dang , Jun Hu , Aaqib Saeed
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13510](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13510)
- **Abstract**:
> Clinical prognosis requires integrating structured measurements, physiological waveforms, longitudinal history, and population context. We introduce PRISM (Population-Retrieved Inference for Structured Multimodal Prognosis), a multimodal language model that combines per-feature numerical tokenization, recency-weighted aggregation of longitudinal ECGs, and a dual-modality population memory bank. Retrieval operates in representation space: the model receives mean-pooled embeddings of similar training-cohort encounters, while no individual retrieved record enters the prompt. On the 1,443-task MDS-ED benchmark, PRISM achieves AUROCs of 86.2% for diagnosis, 93.1% for deterioration, 91.8% for ICU admission, and 92.8% for mortality. These results use a 96-hour pre-arrival window unavailable to reported baselines. With benchmark-window inputs only, PRISM reaches 0.8895 AUROC, compared with 0.8937 for UniPACT and 0.8889 for MDS-ED. A matched-input compact fusion model using the same five evidence streams and frozen encoders reaches 0.7652 AUROC, versus 0.9092 for PRISM. Replacing numerical EHR encoding with text serialization reduces AUROC by 3.66 points; removing pre-arrival history reduces it by 1.97 points; and zeroing retrieval reduces it by 0.99 points. Genuine retrieval outperforms random-neighbor retrieval by 0.97 macro-average points, although a paired task-level sign test does not establish a uniform advantage (p=0.067). On the external MC-MED cohort, supervised fine-tuning reaches 0.8611 AUROC, compared with 0.8227 for locally trained gradient boosting and 0.7841 for source-only PRISM. These results support structure-aware multimodal integration and supervised external adaptation.

## 8. Humanize MLE Agents: Multi-Agent Session Alternation Beyond Single-Agent Limits
- **Authors**: Changye Li , Zijian Zhang , Muheng Li , Yiduo Pang , Tingjun Wu , Menghan Li , Yitong Liu , Hongkai Guo , Mengchi Chen , Chenyu He , Kaisheng Ma , Wei Xu , Song Han , Sihao Liu , Ligeng Zhu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13494](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13494)
- **Abstract**:
> Existing LLM-based agent approaches for long-horizon machine learning engineering (MLE) often use context engineering, implemented through custom harnesses that manage tools, skills, and accumulated experience. However, we find that as single-session execution lengthens, the agent's reasoning intensity declines alongside slowing progress, summarized as long-horizon deceleration, and the benefits of these custom harnesses vanish as the underlying model improves, summarized as harness staleness. These findings suggest that their benefits may not persist across versions, which motivates a workflow-level approach in which agents build on one another's work across sessions. We model these alternating agent sessions as a context-reset semi-Markov decision process (CR-SMDP) and establish sufficient conditions for alternation to exceed single-agent reference limits, derive an explicit experiment-budget bound, and characterize when session caps preserve this advantage. Guided by this analysis, we introduce Humanize MLE Agents (HMA), which alternates two agents on a shared workspace, limits each session to at most k experiments and resets the context between sessions, without additional task-specific harness or prior knowledge supplements. Across 75 Kaggle tasks in MLE-bench, HMA reaches a state-of-the-art any-medal rate of 78.2%, exceeding the strongest open-source baseline by 8.0 points with a quarter of its time budget. Moreover, among six configurations spanning five heterogeneous agent pairs, HMA improves any-medal rate over the constituent baselines by 6.4 points on average, demonstrating cross-pair generality. A supplementary case on kernel optimization reaches 878 cycles, within 1.6% of the community leaderboard leader, demonstrating applicability beyond MLE.

## 9. A Local Agentic AI Framework to Generate Edge Case Scenarios for Safety Assurance

- **Abstract**:

The rapid advancement in the generation and analysis capabilities of language models (LMs) has expanded their applicability and recent work has investigated LM scenario generation. ## 10. SqueezeBench: Saturation-Resilient Evaluation of Mathematical Capability
- **Authors**: Pablo Manrique , Hai Xia , Stefan Szeider
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13461](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13461)
- **Abstract**:
> AI systems are making rapid progress in mathematics, but this progress is hard to measure. Benchmarks of solved problems saturate, and in benchmarks of open problems each problem can be resolved for the first time only once. We present SqueezeBench, a benchmark that is resilient to saturation: a success raises the bar instead of removing the problem. Each problem fixes an integer function $f(n)$ whose values are known only for small $n$, and the benchmark keeps a lower and an upper record on it. A record is a computable Lean function with a machine-checked proof that it bounds $f$. A submission proposes a new function and proves that it bounds $f$, that it is no worse than the current record at any $n$, and that it is strictly better at some $n$. Once accepted, it becomes the record that the next submission has to beat. The system thus chooses the bound as well as its proof, and it may improve the record at a single $n$ or its growth rate. At every $n$, the gap between the two records shows how far the certified bounds are from determining $f(n)$. Every proof is checked by two independent Lean kernels, so no referee needs to check the mathematics. The checks also reject a function that computes $f$ by exhaustive search at small $n$, and a maintainer only reads the submitter's report and looks for such a search at larger $n$. We release ten problems from extremal combinatorics with twenty proved initial records and the admission tools. As a baseline, agents based on a frontier language model, with at most one hour per record, had 82 submissions accepted and improved every record, without going beyond the published literature. Agents based on two newer models then started from these records, had 60 more submissions accepted and improved 19 of the 20 records again. For eight of the ten problems, at least one record is still asymptotically behind the best published bound.

## 12. Rollout-Time Intervention: Unlocking Agentic Reinforcement Learning Beyond Policy Limits

- **Abstract**:
> Reinforcement learning (RL) has emerged as a key paradigm for improving large language model (LLM) agents, enabling stronger reasoning, planning, and tool-use in interactive environments. We introduce Rollout-Time Intervention (RTI), a technique designed to elicit successful trajectories beyond the policy's intrinsic exploration capacity. ## 15. BoundaryEvo: Data-Model Co-Evolution at the Capability Boundary for Long-Chain Reasoning Distillation
- **Authors**: Jiaming Su , Suorong Yang , Xudong Li , Tengchao Yang , Peng Ye
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13285](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13285)
- **Abstract**:
> Distilling long-chain reasoning trajectories from large teacher models provides an effective way to improve the reasoning ability of small language models. However, existing methods typically select a fixed training set before distillation, overlooking that the usefulness of a reasoning example changes as the student evolves. This mismatch becomes especially costly when only a small number of teacher trajectories can be used for training. Besides, examples already mastered provide little new supervision, while examples far beyond the capability of student may be difficult to learn from. Training data should therefore adapt as model capability evolves, yet reliable data selection is difficult when the student is initially weak. To address these challenges, our key idea is to first establish the basic reasoning capability and then adapt the training data to the evolving capability boundary of the student. We propose BoundaryEvo, a two-stage data--model co-evolution framework. In Capability Priming, we directly distill a small set of teacher-generated long reasoning trajectories without using a separate model to judge and filter them, allowing the student to acquire an initial reasoning capability before capability-aware data selection. In Capability-Boundary Co-Evolution, we repeatedly select questions that the current student has not mastered but the expert can reliably solve, and update the training data as the student evolves. With only 1,000 training examples, BoundaryEvo consistently outperforms static and dynamic data selection baselines across 11 reasoning benchmarks, raising the original Qwen3.5-4B accuracy from 62.9 to 75.3 on IMO-AnswerBench and from 26.8 to 46.0 on AMO-Bench. The code and data will be made publicly available.

## 16. Forgetting is Not Always Bad: A Neuro-Inspired Memory Repair Mechanism for Poisoned LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Lei Liu , Yunji Liang , Xiaowen Zhang , Jingqi Liu , Qi Li , Bin Guo , Zhiwen Yu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13267)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language model (LLM) agents are highly vulnerable to memory-poisoning attacks. Existing defenses primarily rely on external modules for either static memory isolation or continuous online auditing, resulting in low memory efficiency and high computational overhead. To address these limitations, inspired by neuroscientific mechanisms that weak reactivation can destabilize memories and induce decay in the absence of reinforcement, we propose an agent-intrinsic training-free memory repair framework, DREAM (Dynamic Reactivation for Engram Attenuation in Memory), that enables selective functional forgetting of poisoned memories. Extensive experiments across diverse attackers and real-world tasks demonstrate that DREAM reduces attack success rates by over 95\% against backdoor poisoning while preserving strong benign utility. DREAM also achieves competitive robustness against injection attacks. In terms of runtime and token consumption, DREAM achieves up to a 2.13$\times$ speedup and a 37.7\% reduction in token consumption compared with A-MemGuard. Furthermore, DREAM also achieves a task success rate of 92.96\% on poisoned multi-agent systems.

## 18. RoleWorld: Knowledge Transfer in World Modeling with Functional Role Abstractions

## 19. Quality, Not Just Outcome: A Structural Scoring Framework for Selecting Coding-Agent Trajectories for Supervised Fine-Tuning
- **Authors**: Sida Li , Yuanxin Liu , Shicheng Li , Linli Yao , Xu Sun , Haoli Bai , Xiaohui Li
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13152)
- **Abstract**:
> Open coding agents are commonly trained through supervised fine-tuning on interaction trajectories whose final patches pass the benchmark test suite. However, this outcome-level filtering criterion ignores process quality: trajectories that eventually succeed may still contain inefficient interaction patterns such as repetitive file inspection, unstable tool usage, or delayed modification behavior, all of which become part of the imitation target. We propose TQS (Trajectory Quality Score), a structural scoring framework for coding-agent trajectories that operates directly on trajectory structure, requires no LLM judge, and runs in linear time. Models trained on top-ranked trajectories consistently outperform those trained on random or bottom-ranked subsets on SWE-Bench-Verified. Our results suggest that process-level trajectory quality is an important factor in coding-agent supervision and that structural trajectory filtering provides an effective and scalable alternative to LLM-based data selection.

## 20. VIGOR: Verifiable Industrial Geometry Optimization and Repair in Scene Programs
- **Authors**: Fuqiang Wang , Zheng Guo , Xiaoming Wu , Hanlin Kong , Nayu Liu , Chuanting Zhang , Daojun Liang , Jingxuan Wei
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13145)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) increasingly generate structured layouts and executable scene programs from natural-language instructions. However, executability does not imply engineering feasibility: an industrial scene program may execute successfully while its final state still violates coupled boundary, collision, clearance, aisle, or structural constraints. We identify this mismatch as the executability-engineering-feasibility gap, where locally plausible placements can remain globally infeasible because corrections to one device may introduce violations elsewhere. To make this gap measurable in a controlled setting, we construct an execution-grounded industrial scene-program dataset with 1,749 constructible scenarios, complete executable programs, and 94 final-state geometric checks per scenario. We further propose VIGOR, which combines supervised scene-program generation with execution-grounded verification and staged geometric refinement. Under teacher forcing, we compare the token likelihood from a frozen reference model with and without access to the task input while keeping the gold solution prefix fixed. The resulting conditional information gain quantifies how much the task itself reduces uncertainty about the demonstrated decision. We then convert this information into a KL-regularized projection of the current policy and derive an equivalent scalar-weighted SFT objective whose logit gradient exactly matches that of the projected target. Across mathematical reasoning and code-generation benchmarks, CIG-SFT consistently outperforms standard SFT and recent adaptive fine-tuning baselines, with particularly strong improvements in generalization to challenging evaluation distributions, while requiring neither additional training data nor online rollouts.
