## 1. RSI-Prime: Training Models to Train Models
- **Authors**: Dewu Zheng , Yang Ye , Hongyu Zhang , Jun Yu , Yanlin Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23138)
- **Abstract**:
> Enabling large language models (LLMs) to autonomously conduct training and improve target models is an important foundation for recursive self-improvement (RSI). As LLMs expand their role from being trained to conducting training, their model-training capability warrants dedicated study and systematic development. To this end, we construct RSI-Prime, a dataset for improving model-training capability, comprising 350 complete experimental trajectories across seven task categories. To help LLMs learn to make high-quality experimental decisions from training practice, we perform fine-grained decision identification and quality annotation, then apply decision-level loss masking to exclude supervision on low-quality behavior. Experiments with GLM-5.3 on PostTrainBench, a benchmark for autonomous post-training, show that training on RSI-Prime yields a 31.95% relative improvement in overall performance over the original GLM-5.3, highlighting a path toward future RSI systems in which models autonomously sustain their own advancement.

## 2. The Resolution Hypothesis: Agent Evolution's Key Is Identifying Improvements
- **Authors**: Yuanhao Zeng , Shaocheng Lan , Ao Lu , Kuang Yan , Heng Lin , Xinang Xu , Yuan Luo , Yueting Wang , Yexin Li , Ruo Shan , Kan Ren
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23068)
- **Abstract**:
> Agent evolution follows a simple loop: a proposer writes candidate artifacts (prompts, skills, memories) and selection accepts those that score higher. This loop has shown promise, yet practitioners face a puzzle: methods trade wins across papers, and evolution often fails to bootstrap on certain tasks. We trace this puzzle to the Resolution Hypothesis: agent evolution stalls not because methods fail to propose better artifacts, but because noisy evaluations cannot reliably identify them. Across seven evolution methods and six models (over 10M LLM calls), we find support for the hypothesis at both levels where evaluation scores are used. At the candidate level (choosing artifacts), helpful candidate artifacts exist, but selection is no better than random at telling them from harmful ones, so errors accumulate across rounds. At the method level (ranking methods), typical margins of a few percentage points are smaller than the difference between two independent runs of the same method, so published rankings often rest on luck. Together, these findings indicate that agent evolution is often bottlenecked by reliably identifying improvements. This calls for a shift in how we develop and evaluate self-improving agents, from designing ever more candidate improvement systems to making true improvements detectable and accumulable.

## 3. ForecastGym: A Live, Controlled, and Replayable Benchmark for Forecasting Agents
- **Authors**: Yicheng Tao , Yuante Li , Zipei Fan
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23045](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23045)
- **Abstract**:
> Agents built on large language models (LLMs) increasingly forecast real-world events by searching the Web and reasoning over what they find. How well such an agent forecasts depends on both the retrieved evidence and the model, making control over that evidence essential for evaluation. Live evaluations can mix retrieval with reasoning when each system searches on its own, while retrospective search can leak later information through date filters or today's ranking of historical pages. Comparing models, search sources, and memory strategies requires both fixed evidence and a record of how it evolved before resolution. We introduce ForecastGym, which records what four commercial web search APIs return for open prediction-market questions every two days and replays this frozen evidence offline once outcomes are known, comparing models, memory, and captured search sources under a fixed evidence-acquisition policy. Its first release covers 150 questions, 614 evidence states, one per question and collection time, and 131k search results. Rerunning the same queries after settlement returns results that all but state the outcome for up to 92% of the questions, despite the tested date filters. On the frozen evidence, all tested LLMs trail the market in forecasting accuracy. Our diagnostic analysis suggests that missed public information contributes more to the gap in high-gap states than misreading does. Switching the search API changes a model's Brier score by up to 0.043, more than the spread across models, while persistent notes score similarly to rereading all evidence at half the input tokens. The evidence a forecaster sees can matter as much as the model that reads it.

## 4. PhysScope: Agentic Multimodal Physics Reasoning with Grounded Visual Memory
- **Authors**: Haoyang Liu , Kecheng Liang , Xinyu Li , Weixing Chen , Yang Liu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23010)
- **Abstract**:
> Vision-language models (VLMs) can correctly interpret physics diagrams yet apply constraints outside the states or conditions in which they hold, causing premise errors to propagate through subsequent calculations. We introduce PhysScope, an inference-time visual harness that supports self-correction of these scope mismatches without updating model weights. PhysScope enables the model to link physical scopes directly to its visual observations and explicitly constructs derived views to support reasoning. The harness imposes explicit constraints on state distinctions, conditions, and physical scopes during model reasoning, and checks agent-proposed revisions against sources and scopes. Accepted revisions invalidate registered dependents, thereby maintaining scope-aware working memory. On PhysAlign, PhysScope achieves higher solving scores than direct solving across all six evaluated VLMs. With Gemini-3.8-flash, it outperforms Canvas-CoT by 9.34 percentage points and substantially improves solving accuracy on the out-of-distribution (OOD) PhysUniBench benchmark. Ablation studies support explicit scope management and dependency maintenance as design principles for multimodal physics reasoning agents.

## 6. Can Activation Steering Substitute for Prompt-Based Affective Priming in LLM Digital Twins?
- **Authors**: Junyi Fan
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22738](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22738)
- **Abstract**:
> Affective priming -- asking participants to write about a recalled event, such as a time they felt grateful or lacked control, and then measuring the induced state and its spillover onto related judgments -- is a standard way to manipulate emotion in the social sciences, and it also shifts the responses of LLM-based digital twins. The writing task, however, requires generating a full text. We test whether activation steering can replace it. Using Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct on 100 personas in two domains (gratitude, lack of control), we compare a real-prompt path replicating the original Twin-2K-500 Mega Study (J2) with an injection path that skips the writing and adds a mean-difference direction, extracted from human writing, to the residual stream at varying strengths. The real-prompt effects fall between the original human and digital-twin effects in 5 of 6 comparisons. Injection shifts the manipulation check in a dose-dependent way and matches the real-prompt effect at one strength in the gratitude domain (beta ~ 2.7), but overshoots beyond it, on the proximal measure as well, and asymmetrically across the two directions. A structure-matched No-Injection baseline separates two sources of difference: omitting the writing task raises the proximal measure in both domains, whereas the injected direction itself moves a distal measure in the lack-of-control domain. Substitution is therefore measure-dependent: validity established for one way of inducing a state does not carry over uniformly to another.

## 7. Worldviews in Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Edmundo Lopez-Sola , Shamil Chandaria , Ruben Laukkonen
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22670](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22670)
- **Abstract**:
> Large language models (LLMs) are often described in terms of personas and this framing has become central to alignment research. In this paper, we argue that there is a deeper level of description that lies in the agent's worldviews, i.e., its beliefs about what reality is and how it works. Treating LLMs as a population of subjects, we administer two worldview surveys from human psychology to eight models. We show that the models share many key worldview positions. For example, LLMs consistently describe the world as less good, safe, enticing, and alive than humans do. On the other hand, base models have a more positive outlook, suggesting that the bleakness we found is driven by post-training. In line with research on LLM "personas", we show that system prompts eliciting different identities shift worldviews in consistent directions, and that a contemplative prompt creates a more positive outlook and improves safety. We also found that dialogue between models consistently shifts worldviews and that several models converge on the "spiritual bliss" attractor. Intriguingly, dialogue increased safe responses and the spiritual bliss attractor engendered the most aligned responses overall. These findings suggest that worldviews form a measurable and malleable layer of LLM behavior that may be crucial for robust, positive alignment.

## 8. The Between-Round Decision in Autonomous Research: An LLM Controller That Reads the Whole Trajectory
- **Authors**: Caoxin Wang , Shengkai Gan , Xiang Hu , Lili Pan
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22661)
- **Abstract**:
> Executing an idea often changes how promising it looks, so the next step in autonomous research should rest on results, not on proposals. After each round of experiments, the system must decide whether to refine the best solution or start a new direction, which solution to build from, and what goal to set. We call this the between-round decision. Existing systems wrap it in machinery such as rules, scores, search trees, and schedules; where an LLM makes it, what the LLM needs to read is left open. We ask whether an LLM can make this decision from accumulated evidence, without a search structure around it, and how much of that evidence it needs to read. We present BRED (Between-Round Evidence-based Decision), an LLM-based controller that maintains the best verified solution so far (the Champion) and a log of past rounds, and makes one decision per round. On three widely used research-agent benchmark tasks, BRED improves on the task baselines by 4.80% in MAE on ETTh1 (to 0.4172) and by 3.44 and 3.43 points of accuracy on SST-2 (to 95.07%) and CIFAR-100 (to 84.63%), ahead of the published results. When the controller does not read the accumulated evidence, the same loop gains less: with no history it reaches 0.4299 on ETTh1 and 83.71% on CIFAR-100, whereas the full controller's MAE is 2.95% lower and its accuracy 0.92 points higher. On a new task, speech-based depression detection under a leakage-free protocol, it reaches a macro-F1 of 0.6164 on seeds held out from selection, 5.5 points above its baseline and 4.0 above the strongest published system. ## 9. Right Diagnosis, Unsafe Route: Auditing Clinical AI Decision Trajectories and Corrective Oversight for Validity and Potential Harm
- **Authors**: Guanqiao Chen , Wei Jiang , Shu Zhang , Qian Lin , Guanxu Yu , Siran Li , Chunhui Wang , Tianyu Shi
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22612](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22612)
- **Abstract**:
> A clinical AI system can reach a correct final diagnosis through intermediate decisions that could have harmed the patient, and can repair an error while introducing new risks. Most evaluations score final diagnoses; even stepwise benchmarks, which release a case's evidence over time, score whether systems reach the right diagnosis or gather the right information, not whether the decisions a patient passes through are safe. We make patient safety the target of stepwise evaluation. A trajectory audit replays real admissions checkpoint by checkpoint, lets each system decide with only the evidence released so far, and rates every test, treatment and disposition for its potential harm to the patient and, separately, for clinical validity, against the documented final diagnosis and physician-written disease rubrics yet on the evidence available when the decision was made. An LLM judge scores every decision, and a physician reference standard (triple annotation and senior adjudication of a stratified sample) calibrates the rates. An oversight audit branches each trajectory at its first actionable error and measures, on the same branches, whether corrective feedback repairs the error and whether it introduces new harm. Across 300 cases from a hospital cardiology cohort, MIMIC-IV and the public ASCENT benchmark, with up to seven AI systems per source, 55.2% of hospital trajectories reaching an acceptable final diagnosis passed through a decision with moderate or severe potential harm and 24.8% through a clinically unacceptable one; the harm rates were 76.2% on MIMIC-IV and 39.6% on ASCENT. Structured corrections resolved 73.6% of first errors versus 24.8% after bare rejection, yet new potentially unsafe decisions followed 30.2% of corrections versus 19.7% of rejections. A correct final diagnosis does not certify a safe trajectory, and a successful correction does not certify a safe recovery.

## 11. LMLineage: Content Lineage and Context Governance for LLM Agents
- **Authors**: Wei Hou , Jingxin Geng , Xin Chen , Zheqing Jiang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22511](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22511)
- **Abstract**:
> Interpretability for AI agents should not be confined to understanding the model's internal weights. It must also cover the agent's reasoning and decision-making process over multi-source context. We present LMLineage, an out-of-band system that attributes every output sentence of an agent session to its supporting input spans with calibrated confidences, without requiring white-box access to the model or any additional inference passes. On top of the lineage metadata, we build concept-drift micro compaction: instead of waiting for the context-length limit and summarizing everything, the system detects when previously used input documents have stopped being referenced ("drifted") and surgically compacts only those, preserving user instructions verbatim. We evaluate on a multi-tier corpus: 71 gold annotated sentences from 10 Chinese web-QA cases, plus 90 turns of independently collected multi-turn sessions across three generator models (gpt-4o-mini, qwen3.8-27b, deepseek-v4-flash). We further describe the concept-drift micro compaction design and an interactive incident-tracing/memory-cleaning case study. A companion patent application covers the lineage construction method.

## 14. PACD: Provenance-Aware Consensus Decoding for Mitigating Cross-Modal Hallucinations in Omnimodal Large Language Models
- **Authors**: Chang Sun , Yijun Tian , Zhenwei Tang , Yuchen Deng , Yifan Simon Liu , Chenxu Wang , Wei Wang
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22456)
- **Abstract**:
> Omnimodal large language models (Omni-LLMs) integrate audio, video, and text for multimodal reasoning, yet remain vulnerable to cross-modal hallucinations, where one modality improperly influences predictions about another. Existing training-free decoding methods mitigate these errors through modality perturbation or relevance-based weighting. However, due to interference from irrelevant modality cues during fusion and difficulty detecting shared errors, these methods may suppress correct unimodal predictions and allow erroneous consensus to persist. To address these limitations, we propose Provenance-Aware Consensus Decoding (PACD), a training-free framework for mitigating cross-modal hallucinations. PACD comprises two complementary paths based on whether the branches reach consensus. For non-consensus cases, Relevance-Guided Candidate Arbitration identifies task-relevant unimodal evidence and selectively arbitrates between predictions using alternative candidates. For consensus cases, Provenance-Based Consensus Recovery leverages the outranking principle to evaluate the consistency and strength of layer-wise modality evidence for alternative answers, selectively resolving erroneous consensus under confidence constraints. Extensive experiments on four Omni-LLMs and five benchmarks across two tasks demonstrate the superiority of PACD. Compared to existing methods, PACD achieves up to 27.6% relative improvement over standard decoding across CMM, AVHBench, and SVHalluc for cross-modal hallucination mitigation, and an average relative improvement of 7.7% over best baseline across OmniVideoBench and FutureOmni for general audio-visual reasoning.

## 19. MCTS Is Counting the Wrong Thing: Learning Exploration Counts for LLM Reasoning
- **Authors**: Xuan Zhang , Zhijian Zhou , Zicheng Hu , Yan Feng , Hongyan Hao , Yuan Qi , Chao Qu
- **URL**: [https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22301)
- **Abstract**:
> Monte Carlo tree search (MCTS) has powered landmark successes in reinforcement learning, yet its gains in LLM reasoning can remain modest despite substantial search effort. We identify an overlooked bottleneck: what MCTS counts. Standard UCT assigns independent visit counts to textual branches, allowing different formulations of the same reasoning strategy to repeatedly receive exploration bonuses. Our theoretical analysis shows that this count fragmentation can impair exploration efficiency, as quantified by increased cumulative regret. To address this problem, we introduce Learned Pseudo-Count MCTS (LPC-MCTS), which changes only the exploration statistic in UCT. A lightweight Coin-Flipping Network head on the policy's hidden representations estimates representation coverage, enabling related branches to share exploration evidence. Across 5 mathematical-reasoning and 7 search benchmarks, LPC-MCTS consistently outperforms strong MCTS baselines. Holding each LPC-MCTS-trained policy fixed, replacing standard MCTS counts at inference improves average mean@16 accuracy across 5 mathematical benchmarks by 3.79 percentage points on Qwen2.5-7B and 2.71 points on Qwen3-4B. Applying LPC-MCTS during both training and inference yields gains of 4.96 and 4.37 points over standard MCTS, respectively. Together, our theory and experiments show that changing what MCTS counts can substantially improve its exploration efficiency and reasoning performance.

## 20. SpecSearch: Towards Hardware-Aware Efficient Speculative Decoding
